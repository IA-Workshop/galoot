## 4. Acceptance criteria

1. **Kernel associativity.** The geometric product and the wedge are associative, and the scalar 1 is the identity of the geometric product on both sides, on seeded random integer multivectors over every group of `identities_checks.hpp`'s `groups` list, on the layouts each group names (today these hold only on rigid(3) and conformal(3), `product_test.cpp:247`).
2. **The defining relation at the kernel.** For random vectors a and b of every group, a ⟑ b + b ⟑ a is the scalar 2 Σ a_i g_ij b_j, with the right side computed in the test from `Alg::value.gram`, independently of `dual.hpp`.
3. **The remaining product rules.** antireverse(a ∨ b) = antireverse(b) ∨ antireverse(a); grade_involution(a ∧ b) = grade_involution(a) ∧ grade_involution(b); grade_involution(a ⟇ b) relates to the grade involutions of a and b by the sign the math-expert derives from 2.1 (the antigrade involution is (−1)^n times the grade involution, so the expected rule depends on n; part A states it before the test is written). Over every group.
4. **Exhaustive basis checks through the public functions.** For each group whose layout is `full<n>` (n ≤ 5: the forms up to three, of four, the diagonal forms of five, and conformal(3)), each bilinear identity of criteria 1 to 3 and of the De Morgan laws holds on every pair of basis blades, and each associativity law on every triple, by run-time loops over full-layout multivectors with one nonzero coefficient, so that no new template is instantiated. This is a proof (V1 kind 1) through `mv`, the layouts, and their orientations, not only through the plans.
5. **Seeded random diagonal signatures (section 5, last bullet).** A group of R(p, q, r) with n = 5 and 6, drawn by the math-expert from a seed recorded in the source's comment, that includes at least one draw with q ≥ 2 and r ≥ 2 and one with r ≥ 3. Each family of criteria 1 to 3, the De Morgan laws, the antiproduct laws, and the dual identities of `dual_identities_test.cpp` runs on it, on vectors and bivectors at n = 6 and on full layouts at n = 5 where the compilers' default limits allow (part A predicts with `snippets/plan_cost_model`, part C confirms).
6. **Golden values (O2).** If the owner takes the recommendation: `golden.py` gains namespaces for 3.3 (the four duals of every basis blade of rigid(3) and conformal(3)) and 4.4 (the odd sign −V ⟇ x̌ ⟇ V̰ for a reflection in n = 3, 4, and 5, the values `conventions_check.py` already checks), each with a `// Fact:` line that the trace table holds, and C++ tests compare the library with them. `reference.golden` (the regenerate-and-diff test) passes.
7. **Section 8.** If the owner ratifies the wording (O5), the "Dual sign" row's Test column names "the duals of a point are planes with the dual-sign convention" (`dual_test.cpp:961`), and the "Conformal duals" row names "the bulk dual of a conformal sphere is a round point" (`dual_test.cpp:1122`) only if the math-expert confirms that the conformal wiki's Duals table would fail it. ## 13. Risks

1. **Compile cost.** The identities sources are the critical path of every build (decision 0189). Adding associativity of ⟑ to the dense five and six groups instantiates products of three bivectors, which their own comment says one compiler cannot evaluate at its defaults. Mitigation: part A's prediction, the per-identity layouts the dense group already uses, and criterion 14's record. A group that cannot build at the defaults gets its own source under a stated limit, as decision 0193 did, not a narrower test.
2. **Run time.** Criterion 4's triples at n = 5 are 32,768 per form, each a few products of 1,024-term plans. Under the sanitizers that may take minutes. Mitigation: `core.identities` is already `slow`; the implementer measures and may move the exhaustive loops to a source of their own with the same label.
3. **The random R(p, q, r) group hits a limit.** Mitigated as risk 1.
4. **The scan as a test depends on CTest's listing format** (`json-v1`) and on running `ctest --show-only` from inside a test; a preset's own test filter (gcc-asan, clang-measure) must not hide compile-fail tests from the listing. The test reads the listing of its own tree without the preset's filter.
5. **Common-mode error.** The math-expert writes the golden additions and the predictions; the test author writes the tests; the reviewer recomputes by its own route. Agreement between agents of one kind is weak evidence (plan 0001 risk 5); the kind-2 identities and the owner's reading of the reference script remain the mitigation.
6. **A seed or a new identity finds a defect.** It is reported (W9), kept as a fixed test (V7), and fixed here only if small and in scope; otherwise the unit halts for that item (W8).
7. **WU12 reshapes the suite later.** The coverage table and the group structure are what WU12 extends; WU14 adds no change-of-basis helper that WU12 would have to undo.
