## 1. Departures, ambiguities, and owner questions (C20)

1. Two units, not one. Plan 0001 lists WU6 as one unit of size M. The blade-set unit (plan 0008, size M) ran to a 600-line header, 860 lines of tests, 35 rejection tests, and two fix passes, and its first review still missed a defect (plan 0009). The pair function and the plan over sets are separable, each with its own tests and its own review in one sitting (W2). This plan covers both as WU6a and WU6b, sequential, each merged to `develop` on its own. The owner may collapse them into one branch; the acceptance criteria do not change.
2. Namespace. Decision 0015 keeps plan term lists in `detail` in v1, with no stability promise, and tests may use them. The reading followed: `blade_product`, `term_list`, `plan_term`, `gp_plan`, and the capacity helpers live in `galoot::core::detail`; only `coefficient_type` is a public name, because the multivector unit's scalar conversion and DoD-7 refer to it. If the owner wants `blade_product` public now, it moves up without changing its signature; nothing in this plan depends on the choice.
3. Coefficient type and overflow (DoD-7; note 0009, open question 4; decision 0035 item 2 leaves it to this unit). Proposed: `coefficient_type` is a single alias for `std::int64_t`, as `entry_type` is for `int` (decision 0036: no type parameter until a second type has a user), and every multiplication and addition of coefficients during generation is guarded by a `GALOOT_ASSERT` whose bound is derived from `std::numeric_limits<coefficient_type>::max()`. In constant evaluation the compiler already rejects signed overflow, but with its own message; the guard gives the message in the algebra's terms (C9) and also catches overflow when the generator runs at run time in a checking build. Its cost is measured in WU6a and reported; the owner may drop it by a decision if the cost is notable. The alternative, `int`, overflows at n = 4 for a dense form with entries near `max_form_entry`; the review of note 0009 showed the coefficient is an integer polynomial of degree (|A| + |B| − |C|)/2 in the g_ij, so no entry bound makes `int` safe.
4. Golden tables. Full product tables of four signatures (about 1,400 lines) are added to `test/reference/golden.hpp`, which every test that uses golden values compiles. The alternative, a CTest that runs `clifford_reference.py table` and diffs it against a C++ program's output, would keep the header small but put Python on the test's run-time path. Proposed: the header, with its cost measured (track-only) and the second route left as a fallback if the cost is judged too high.
5. Boundary with WU7. Plan 0001 gives WU7 "result typing, unary maps, complement conjugation, memoization". Here the plan's terms carry the output blade as a canonical mask; the result blade set in canonical order and the rewriting of `out` as an index are WU7's. The memoizing form (an inline `constexpr` variable template with a `consteval` function-template initializer, note 0011) is fixed here, because the plan cannot exist without a form; WU7 adds the Clang evaluation-count check. The task statement for this plan named "blade_product and the product plan" and that is the reading followed.
6. First P2 run. The M2 exit evidence names a first P2 run. WU6b takes it as a recorded measurement over fixed use at n = 4, 8, 16 (below), under `docs/agent/records/performance/`, not as a test and not as DoD-23 in full, which stays with WU17.
7. Witt bases. Plan 0001 section 5 lists "Witt bases with one and two fermion modes". With integer Gram entries and e_i e_j + e_j e_i = 2 g_ij, the relation {a, a†} = 1 needs g = 1/2, which the metric cannot hold (note 0009, B5: half-integer forms give dyadic coefficients). The reading followed, matching decision 0060's "scaled Witt" wording: the Witt forms are [[0, 1], [1, 0]] and its two-mode block form, with {a, a†} = 2, and the DoD-16 "CAR relations" are checked up to that scale. 8. Exact rational. WU5 moved to M5 (decision 0094). ## 2. Content

In `galoot::core` (public) and `galoot::core::detail` (term-list machinery, decision 0015):

### WU6a: `include/galoot/core/blade_product.hpp`

- `coefficient_type`, an alias for `std::int64_t`: the exact coefficient of a generated term (C7). One alias, no template parameter.
- `detail::term`: a structural aggregate of a blade mask (`mask_t blade`) and a `coefficient_type coefficient`, with defaulted `==`.
- `detail::term_list<Capacity>`: a structural, trivially copyable aggregate of `std::array<term, Capacity> terms` and `std::size_t size`, with `add(mask_t, coefficient_type)` and `size()`. `add` inserts a term in canonical blade order (grade, then index list, the order of `canonical_less`), merges a term whose blade is already listed, and removes a term whose coefficient becomes zero, so that two lists of the same product compare equal with `==`. It asserts, with `GALOOT_ASSERT`, that the list does not outgrow `Capacity` ("more product terms than the capacity") and that the sum fits `coefficient_type`.
- `detail::product_capacity(mask_t a, mask_t b)`: the largest number of terms e_A e_B can have, 2^(|A ∪ B| − 1), or 1 when both are the scalar. Derivation, stated in the header: every term's blade is a subset of A ∪ B, because each step of the recursion either removes a generator or adds one of A, and every term has grade congruent to |A| + |B| modulo 2, so at most half the subsets of A ∪ B appear.
- `detail::multiply_checked(coefficient_type, coefficient_type)` and the matching addition guard: the result when it fits `coefficient_type`, otherwise a failed `GALOOT_ASSERT` ("a product coefficient does not fit coefficient_type"); the bound is `numeric_limits<coefficient_type>::max()` divided by the magnitude of one factor. No literal appears.
- `detail::blade_product<Capacity, N>(const metric<N>& g, mask_t a, mask_t b)`: the terms of e_A e_B in the wedge-monomial basis as a `term_list<Capacity>`. Requires that `a` and `b` name only e_1 to e_N (asserted with the message `bits.hpp` uses, "blade has a generator beyond e_n") and `Capacity >= product_capacity(a, b)` (the overrun assertion fires otherwise). The direct form from the defining relations, as Spike A's generator (note 0008): for a generator, e_i x = e_i ⌋ x + e_i ∧ x, with e_i ⌋ e_B = Σ_p (−1)^(p−1) g(i, b_p) e_(B ∖ b_p) and e_i ∧ e_B = ± e_(B ∪ i) by the reorder sign; for a blade with lowest index a and rest A′, e_A x = e_a (e_A′ x) − (e_a ⌋ e_A′) x. Integer arithmetic only. The metric is taken by value reference, not as a tag, so the identity suite can run it on signatures built at run time (Spike A, finding 1); the tag enters at the plan.

### WU6b: `include/galoot/core/plan.hpp`

- `detail::plan_term`: a structural aggregate of `std::size_t a` and `b` (positions in the input blade sets), `mask_t out` (the canonical mask of the output blade, sign +1, as decision 0091 fixes for generated results), and `coefficient_type coefficient`, with defaulted `==`. The orientation signs of the two input blades are folded into the coefficient at generation, so a coefficient stored on −e14 multiplies as −e14.
- `detail::generate_gp<Capacity, N>(const metric<N>& g, std::span<const oriented_blade> as, std::span<const oriented_blade> bs, std::span<plan_term> out)`: writes the terms of e_A e_B for every A in `as` and B in `bs`, in order of `a`, then `b`, then canonical order of `out`, writes as many as fit in `out`, and returns the count of all. The counting pass passes an empty span; the contract says so in the `///` comment (C8).
- `detail::plan_capacity(std::span<const oriented_blade>, std::span<const oriented_blade>)`: the largest `product_capacity` over the pairs, the scratch size of the plan.
- `detail::gp_plan<Alg, A, B>`: `template <metric_like Alg, blade_set A, blade_set B> inline constexpr auto gp_plan = detail::make_gp_plan<Alg, A, B>();`, a `std::array<plan_term, T>` with T from the counting pass. `make_gp_plan` is a `consteval` function template (not a lambda: note 0008 finding 5, note 0011 finding 4) that counts in a `constexpr` local and then fills. Before generating, it checks each set once with a `static_assert`: every mask names only e_1 to e_n ("gp plan: a blade set names a generator beyond e_n") and every sign is +1 or −1 ("gp plan: a blade set holds a sign other than +1 or -1"). After generating, it checks what is cheap (V4): every coefficient nonzero, every `a` below `A.size()`, every `b` below `B.size()`, every `out` within n, each a `static_assert`. A repeated mask in a set is not checked here (decision 0092 leaves whole-set checks to the type that stores a set, WU8).

## 4. Acceptance criteria

WU6a:

1. For every signature of the n ≤ 6 set (section 5), `blade_product` satisfies the defining relations on every pair of generators and is associative on every triple of blades, checked at run time by test-side multivector multiplication over the term lists (V1 kind 2, exhaustive, so a proof over the range).
2. For every signature of the set with n ≤ 5 and every blade, the antisymmetrized product Σ_σ sgn(σ) e_(a_σ1) ⋯ e_(a_σk) equals k! e_A, computed through `blade_product` in the test. This is the check that pins the wedge-monomial basis (decision 0003, CONVENTIONS 1.1), which the defining relations and associativity do not.
3. Every pair of every golden table agrees with `blade_product` by `==` on term lists (V1 kind 3), and the nine conformal(3) products of `golden::wedge_basis` agree in a `static_assert`.
4. For every diagonal signature of the set and every pair, `blade_product` equals the single-term rule computed in the test with its own inversion count: the reorder sign times the squares of the common generators times e_(A xor B), or nothing when a common square is zero.
5. The hand-derived values of section 6 (A6) hold, and the pseudoscalar square equals (−1)^(n(n−1)/2) det g for every golden table and every diagonal signature of the set.
6. No product of the set exceeds `product_capacity` of its pair; a capacity that is too small, a mask beyond n, and a coefficient that does not fit `coefficient_type` each fail a constant evaluation with the stated message (compile-fail tests, both compilers) and abort a checking run-time build with the message and `blade_product.hpp:`.
7. `term` and `term_list<K>` are aggregates and trivially copyable, and `term_list` compares equal across a reordering of `add` calls (canonical order), each a `static_assert` with a mutant.
8. The cost of the overflow guard is measured on both compilers (instructions, `-fsyntax-only`, the n = 5 full table in a constant evaluation, with and without the guard) and recorded in decision 0101.

WU6b:

## 7. Approach

Direct form first (C3, W4): Spike A's recursion, which agrees with the reference on every pair of fourteen signatures on both compilers (note 0008), written as library code in the style of `blade_set.hpp`: `constexpr` functions over values, `GALOOT_ASSERT` for preconditions with the message on the assertion's line, `static_assert`s with literal messages in `consteval` code, no catch-all overloads (no function here takes a typed operand; decisions 0060 item 7 and 0090 item 3), no table precomputed. Hybrid template arguments (decision 0021, S7): `gp_plan` takes the algebra as a tag constrained by `metric_like` and reads `Alg::value`; the blade sets are class-type values; the pair function takes the metric value. Nothing optimized before measurement; the P1 and P2 records are where a later unit would find the need.

### How the plan stays within constant-evaluation limits

Each point rests on a measured spike finding; the unit confirms it on the real headers (criterion 14).

1. The exhaustive proofs run at run time with the same `constexpr` function (note 0008, recommendation 1 and open question 3; C12). No preset raises a limit. Compile-time checks are kept to tables of n ≤ 4 (note 0008: the n = 5 full table passes default limits only at -O0 with a 2^n scratch, and note 0011 finding 5 shows -O1 and above fail it) and to the typed and sparse pairs, which are tiny.
2. Scratch is sized from the blade sets, not from n: `plan_capacity` is 2^(max |A ∪ B| − 1) over the pairs. Note 0008 finding 6 measured a 2.7× saving on Clang from a small scratch, because Clang pays to zero-fill every element of every scratch array at every recursion level. For the rga3 typed pairs the capacity is at most 8; for a sparse pair at n = 16 it is at most 2. A too-small capacity fails loudly (A9's rejection), never silently.
3. The memoizing form is an inline `constexpr` variable template with a `consteval` function-template initializer (note 0011: the three forms cost the same, the variable template is the most direct, and a function template avoids GCC's -O2 double evaluation that a plain lambda caused in note 0008 finding 5). Every use reads the variable template; nothing calls the generator directly in a constant expression, because on Clang each direct call is a full evaluation (note 0011 finding 3).
4. The counting pass runs once per specialization in a `constexpr` local of `make_gp_plan`, and the fill once; the fill is the binding evaluation (note 0008, sweep).
5. Loops stay nested per pair and per blade, each at most 2^n ≤ 2^16 iterations, under GCC's loop limit of 2^18 (note 0008: a design that flattens all pairs into one loop would reach it at n = 9). Recursion depth is the grade, at most `mask_bits`, under GCC's depth limit of 512.
6. The guards are cheap: one division per coefficient multiplication; the sign and range checks on a set run once per set as `static_assert`s on the template arguments, not per pair.
7. What is not controlled is stated: the measured largest `full<n>` against itself at default limits on each compiler and configuration goes into the `///` comment and decision 0103, in the form of `full` and `merge`. Galoot's own flags define `_GLIBCXX_ASSERTIONS`, so the figure under them is the one Galoot's tests face; the bare-flag figure is the one a user's default build faces (note 0011 finding 5).
8. Nothing here needs `index_of` (its cost, decision 0093, is WU7's concern when results are typed) or `merge`.

## 11. Risks

1. Common-mode error (plan 0001, risk 5): the reference script and Spike A's generator had one author. Mitigation: the script was reviewed independently (B5), its checks come from definitions, A3 pins the basis by a route neither implementation uses, and the hand values of A6 and the theorem of A7 are a third route.
2. The overflow guard's cost may be notable in constant evaluation. It is measured (criterion 8) and the owner can drop it; without it, overflow in a constant evaluation still fails to compile, with the compiler's own message.
3. Clang's step limit and libstdc++'s assertion steps may put the n = 4 compile-time checks closer to the limit than expected under -O2 with `_GLIBCXX_ASSERTIONS`. If a check fails at default limits, it moves to run time (point 1 above), and nothing is weakened; the header's own uses stay well under.
4. `golden.hpp` grows by about 1,400 lines, raising the compile cost of every test that includes it. Measured; the fallback is departure 4's second route or a second generated header, by a decision.
5. The term count of a plan grows with the form: dense random forms give up to 14 terms per pair at n = 5 (note 0009, B2), so plan sizes and compile cost are form-dependent, not only n-dependent; the P2 record says so.
6. GCC's constexpr call cache makes a memoization defect invisible on GCC (note 0011, finding 3); only the Clang reading of criterion 15 sees it, and until WU7's CTest exists it is a hand step.
7. The exhaustive associativity check over (2^6)^3 triples for every n = 6 signature with dense term lists may take seconds in a debug build; if it slows the edit cycle it carries the `slow` label and runs from the next merge level up (P9), never omitted.
8. The compile-fail lane (plan 0010) may merge during this unit; the rejection tests here use `galoot_add_compile_fail_test` by its unchanged signature, and a merge of `develop` before the review resolves any adjacent edit in `test/CMakeLists.txt`.
9. Lessons 0060 to 0062 apply: a mutation run checks the build's exit status; scripted edits assert their anchors; before-and-after costs come from clean revisions; a lost pool slot is reported.
10. Stack use at run time (added 2026-11-01 after the W5 review of WU6a, finding F10). Each level of `blade_product`'s recursion holds one scratch `term_list<Capacity>`, of Capacity × 16 + 8 bytes, and the recursion is up to the grade deep. At the full capacity of n = 16, 2^15, that is 512 KiB a level: the antiscalar of R(16,0,0) squared at run time with that capacity segfaults at the default 8 MiB stack and runs at 16 MiB (GCC 16.2.1, -O0; `records/performance/stack-probe/`). Constant evaluation is not affected. WU6b's `generate_gp`, the identity suite above n = 6, and any run-time use keep capacities small (`plan_capacity` over the pairs a program uses), or the scratch moves off the stack, by a decision with a measurement.
