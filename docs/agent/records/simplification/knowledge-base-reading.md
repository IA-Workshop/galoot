## Points where a note conflicts with, qualifies, or adds to the plan

1. **Interface size: the plan's 97 and 263 are a superseded draft, and both pairs count templates twice.** (Conflicts with plan section 3.6 and 5.7.) `deep-modules-and-interface-size.md`, `case-study-measurements.md` and `safe-refactoring-evidence.md` now say 110 names and 293 declarations. Their commit `74ef874` explains the change: the first query named the preset namespace `::galoot::rga3`, which matched nothing, and the right name is `::galoot::presets::rga3`. That adds the preset's 13 names and 30 rows. Both pairs come from an AST matcher that lists a function template and the function declaration it describes as two declarations (likewise variable, alias and class templates), and that also lists the instantiations the preset's own definitions cause. Counted once each, the namespace scope holds 153 declarations under 110 names. The README of this directory gives the method and the reconciliation, row by row. The plan's "23 `_layout` companions" is also from the draft: 25 names end in `_layout`, 20 in the core (the result layouts of operations) and 5 the preset's object layouts.

2. **Duplication: 2.78% and 25.6% are not two readings of one quantity.** (Qualifies the plan's section 3.3 and its use of 25.6% in section 5.7 as a measure of a reader's load.) `measuring-complexity.md` and `compositional-design-in-cpp.md` report lizard's rate. lizard renames identifiers consistently within a scope, keeps the pattern of which names repeat, needs at least 70 tokens per copy for a pair, and stops renaming in windows dense with literals. So it finds deliberate wrapper shapes (seven copies of one product wrapper, four of another) and misses the message chains entirely. The plan's measure maps every identifier to one symbol and masks every literal, with a 60-token window. It therefore counts any two stretches of the same syntactic shape, `#include` blocks among them. Its value moves from 68% at a 20-token window to 7% at 150 (`baseline/duplication-by-site.txt`). Neither is wrong. The plan's number is the one its units can move, since 64% of its duplicated tokens lie inside the candidate sites, 23% in E1's and 20% in E2's. But it cannot fall far: 36% of its duplicated tokens are outside every site, include lists and one-line forwarding wrappers among them, and no unit is meant to remove those. The restated targets use it only as a trend, not as a measure of a reader's load.

3. **Comments: "nothing from deleting comments" rests on a block-level heuristic.** (Conflicts with the plan's sections 5.4 and 5.7 as stated in the notes, and is resolved by the crossing below.) `comments-and-self-documentation.md`, `ranked-techniques-for-template-libraries.md` (step 8, "usually low" payoff) and `case-study-measurements.md` ("nothing from deleting comments") conclude that most comment text is contract or reason. The census gives every line of a block the block's class if one keyword appears anywhere in it. Crossed with the plan's position classes (`baseline/comment-census.tsv`):
   - 674 of the 1,122 "why" lines are file prologues, the tutorials with examples that plan unit D1 shortens.
   - 302 of the 1,336 "contract" lines are on `detail` entities, which plan unit D2 reduces.

   So the census does not show that little can be deleted. It shows that most blocks contain a reason or a heading somewhere. The notes say this themselves ("supports only the order of magnitude"). Two points from the note do bear on the plan:
   - It lists what a template library's interface comment must carry beyond a run-time one: which misuses are rejected and with what message, compile cost and how it grows, the compiler limits that bound it, and the rounding order of a result. Under the owner's ruling on S11 (decision 0253 item 14), those items move to the guide's reference pages. Plan 0049's U9 should receive them as contract, not as optional detail.
   - The notes and the plan agree that comments cost nothing to compile, and that process notes do not belong in code.

4. **Clang's step margin of 1.49 is the margin of six sampled programs. The suite already pins tighter margins, and a unit that touches the generator will change pinned counts.** (Qualifies the notes. It adds to the plan's sections 2, 6.6 and 7, and conflicts with its definition-of-done item 4.)
   - **What the notes say.** `compile-time-cost-measurement.md` and `case-study-measurements.md` measured, by bisection with `-fsyntax-only`, the smallest limits at which six translation units compile. Template depth is never a constraint: 14 to 38 against 900 and 1024. Clang's step limit is the tightest: `product_test.cpp` needs 704,512 of 1,048,576 steps, a margin of 1.49, and GCC's margin is about 10. The limit applies per evaluation, so the largest single table sets it.
   - **What the project's own records say.** The test suite has `core.plan_cost_model.<unit>`: eight units whose exact Clang step and GCC operation thresholds are written in `test/CMakeLists.txt` (from line 795). Each is compiled at its threshold and one below, and the test fails if the count moves either way. The guide publishes the same counts (`docs/guide/limits.md`, "Steps and operations of each plan"). They include `right_complement_full8` at 813,090 steps (margin 1.29) and the geometric antiproduct of two `full<5>` multivectors of conformal(3) at 947,839 steps (margin 1.11). Three test sources are compiled with `-fconstexpr-steps=1200000` because that antiproduct exceeds the default without libstdc++'s assertions. So the tightest margin in the suite is below 1.49, and on one configuration it is below 1.
   - **Checking setting.** The notes measured with `GALOOT_CHECKING=0`. That does not change the counts: `detail::checking_now()` returns true in every constant evaluation, so `GALOOT_ASSERT` checks there either way (`assert.hpp:115-120`).
   - **What it means for the plan.**
     - Every unit marked `pinned` in `candidates.tsv` (B5, B1, B6, and C1c if it runs) changes code on the path that `product_plan` or `unary_map` evaluates. If its step or operation count moves, `core.plan_cost_model` fails. That holds even when the count falls, until the thresholds and the guide's counts are worked out again.
     - The units marked `yes` (B2, B3, C1b) change kernel tables that the pinned units do not read. They can still move the margin of the sources that build large kernels.
     - Definition-of-done item 4 says every test of the suite passes unmodified, except retired `internal` tests. The plan-cost tests are neither. Read literally, item 4 forbids any generator unit that changes a count. Read as the test's own comment intends, the thresholds are expected values, and a unit changes them in a separate commit listed for review (W9), with the guide's page in the same unit (W12).
     - Decision 0253 already requires a generator unit to report the margin before and after. The plan-cost tests are where that report comes from: exact counts, on both compilers, already run by the suite.
     - Open item 1 of `restated-targets.md` puts the reading of item 4 to the owner.

5. **Code identity needs `-fno-ipa-icf` on GCC.** (Adds to the plan's section 6.4.) `safe-refactoring-evidence.md` and exercise cpp-cr04 found that at `-O2`, GCC's identical-code folding turns the operator spelling's function into a call to the named one. A comparison of disassembly then shows a difference that is really a proof of identity. With `-fno-ipa-icf` the two are identical, as on Clang. The plan's object-code comparison of `kernels_inline.cpp` normalizes addresses and symbol hashes, but says nothing of folding. B8, E2 and C1 should compare with `-fno-ipa-icf` on GCC, or compare each function's own body. They should count operations, not instructions, across compilers: Clang packs pairs of multiplications that GCC leaves scalar.

6. **Rejection-path tests are the ones most likely to look redundant.** (Agrees with the plan's sections 5.2 and 5.5 and adds a cost note for plan 0047.) `deletion-first-simplification.md` and `safe-refactoring-evidence.md` report the mutation sample of `bits.hpp`: 481 code mutants. Of the 347 that could be killed, 334 died at compile time, 4 at run time, and 9 only by the death tests that check an assertion and its message; 12 were equivalent. The plan already cites this to keep every rejection test. Two further points:
   - A mutation run of constant-evaluated code can mostly stop after `-fsyntax-only`. That bears on the cost of plan 0047's V6 sweep, which plan 0048's units are judged against.
   - E1 and E2 change about 50 literals. The compile-fail tests then change their expected text, and stay.

7. **Wrapper collapse ranks low in the notes, and the notes prefer the spelling in the message.** (Qualifies E1 under the ruled option C. It does not reopen the ruling.)
   - `ranked-techniques-for-template-libraries.md` puts "collapse repeated wrapper boilerplate" seventh of eight: worth doing "only when the number of operations is going to grow", with "messages per spelling must survive" as its evidence.
   - `compositional-design-in-cpp.md` offers "leave it" for a fixed set of wrappers, with a test pinning each spelling's message.
   - `simple-versus-easy.md` names "passing the spelling as a compile-time tag to one mechanism" as the way to keep diagnostic policy apart from mechanism. That is today's `ProductSpelling` design, which E1 deletes.
   - The owner ruled option C (decision 0253 item 15), and the plan's case for it (the fix is the same for every spelling, and the user's own line is in the error) still stands. The notes are a dissent the E1 review should read, not a reason to stop.

8. **Internal names in diagnostics are interface cost.** (Adds to E2's review.) `deep-modules-and-interface-size.md` counts every internal name that appears in an error as interface. Under E2 the one exactness check moves into the kernel. A scalar that cannot hold a coefficient is then reported from inside `detail::apply`, with the public call further down the instantiation notes. Today it is reported from `operator*` or `geometric_product`. The plan's section 6.5 already asks for the full output of ten compile-fail sources before and after. E2's sample should include the exactness rejections on both compilers, read for where `detail::` names appear.

9. **Deriving at the table level costs compile time that grows with the algebra.** (Qualifies C1 and adds to C0's pass rule.) `compositional-design-in-cpp.md` supports deriving at the table level, which is what C1 proposes. It warns that "a table built through complements in all n generators grows with n even when the operands use few of them". The guide gives the case: the same sparse sets cost 3,136 steps through the geometric product at every n, and 77,513 through the antiproduct at n = 16. C1 composes complements into more kernels. C0's pass rule names compile instructions and peak memory, but not step counts. It should add the plan-cost counts and the margin on both compilers (point 4).

10. **The notes rank shrinking the interface first; the owner ruled no 2.0 batch.** (A conflict of judgment, already ruled.) `ranked-techniques-for-template-libraries.md` step 1 calls a smaller interface "the only step that lowers what every user must learn". `deep-modules-and-interface-size.md` adds that collapsing implementation leaves the module no easier to learn. The plan's section 5.6 recommended no 2.0 batch, and the owner ruled so (decision 0253 item 18). Plan 0048 is therefore an implementation simplification by the notes' own definition. Its expected savings are in maintenance, not in what a user learns. `restated-targets.md` says so.

11. **The functions highest by cognitive complexity are the ones B6 and E1 rewrite.** (Adds a measure to the plan's section 5.7.) `measuring-complexity.md` lists clang-tidy's cognitive complexity: maximum 22, none at the default threshold of 25. The top five are `map_defects_of` 22, `unite` 21, `eliminate` 21, `reject_product` 20 and `typed_defects_of` 17 (exercise output `cr01-cognitive.txt`). B6 rewrites two of them and E1 deletes one. The two that stay, `unite` and `eliminate`, are algorithms. The plan already tracks "functions above complexity 10". The cognitive scores of these three are a sharper before-and-after measure.

12. **Points of agreement, for the record.**
    - Keeping the generator's self-checks (O4, `simple-versus-easy.md`: decomplect only what changes for different reasons).
    - The plan's class (e) items on one evaluation per table and on folds (`compositional-design-in-cpp.md`).
    - The cost of forced inlining and long kernels in GCC's back end even at `-O0` (register allocation 21% of the transform tests' time).
    - Named algebras as thin layers on the generic core (`ga-library-prior-art.md`).
    - Characterization before change (`safe-refactoring-evidence.md`). The plan's R0 snapshot is a characterization test at the public boundary. The notes' "golden master from an independent implementation" already exists for the tables in `test/reference/golden.py`.

## What the notes do not cover

None of the notes looks at the generator's self-checks as a cost, at the expression-snapshot idea of R0, or at contract comments moving to a guide (plan 0049). On those the plan stands on its own evidence.
