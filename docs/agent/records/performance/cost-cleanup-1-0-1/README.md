# Where the two costliest test units spend their compile cost

Measured by Claude (agent, Opus 5.5), planner of plan 0043, on 2026-11-16 from 09:42 to 09:55 UTC, in the worktree of `plan/0043` at `c95fee512488` (the code of `main` after the 1.0.0 release; nothing under `include/`, `test/`, `tools/` or `cmake/` differs from the release tag's tree).

## Question

Of `identities_rounding_test.cpp` under clang-measure (602.5 s, 1.81 GiB at the release revision) and `transform_identities_test.cpp` under gcc-debug (5.93 GiB, 92.3 s), which parts of each unit cost what, and does the cost add up over the parts or grow faster than their sum?

## Method

- Variants of each unit compiled alone, each keeping exactly one part of the test case's body, with everything else of the source unchanged. `probe/rounding.patch` wraps each of the twelve check calls of `identities_rounding_test.cpp` in `#if PROBE == k`; `probe/transform.patch` keeps one of the 25 forms of `transform_identities_test.cpp` (22 small, 1 dense, 2 large) with `if constexpr` in a generic lambda; `probe/transform_gated.patch` also gates the dense form, so that `PROBE=-1` measures the headers and the test's frame alone. A second set (`rounding_law.cpp`, generated by wrapping each `compare(` statement of `check_associativity` and `check_de_morgan` in `#if LAW == k`) keeps one law of the two conformal(3) sections. The variants are not committed as sources; the patches against the files at `c95fee5` and the generator steps above reproduce them.
- Compile commands: those of fresh `cmake --preset clang-measure` and `cmake --preset gcc-debug` trees (`ninja -t commands`), with `-Wno-error -DPROBE=<k> [-DLAW=<k>]` added (the variants leave helpers unused, which only warns). Each compile went through `tools/measure tu`, whose rows are `compile.tsv`; `probe/build.ninja` is the Ninja file, with the scratch and worktree paths replaced by placeholders. The original units ran in the same build (`*_whole.o`).
- Two builds through `tools/timed --log runs.tsv build - probe:sections` and `probe:laws`, under `memguard.sh` (stop below 20 GB available; it never fired; peak tree RSS 18.1 GiB and 2.9 GiB, `memguard.tsv`, `memguard-2.tsv`), with the timing lock held through `lock.sh` and the shared pool (23 of 23 free before and after).
- `table.tsv`: one row per object, instructions in 10⁹ (performance plus efficiency cores).

## Conditions

Shared mode, unpinned, 23 jobs at once. An unrelated session held two cores at 100% throughout, and other work raised the load average to 4.1 to 6.1 (`conditions-*.txt`). **Wall times are under load and are not comparable with pinned or idle rows; instructions and peak memory are** (P14).

## Reading

- **The rounding unit's cost adds up over its twelve sections** (sum of the sections less eleven copies of the frame: 4,304 against 4,273 G, 0.7%), and 97% of it is the two conformal(3) sections.
- **Within those two sections it does not add up.** One law at a time, s1 costs 609 G against 1,692 G for its two laws together, and s3 463 G against 2,451 G for its eight. So the cost grows faster than the code within one function body (inferred: the laws of one section are instantiated in one function, which Clang optimizes as a whole at `-O2` with the kernels forced inline; not traced with `-ftime-trace`). Compiled one law per function, the unit's parts total about 1,328 G, 31% of the whole, and the largest part is one ⟇ associativity law, 308 G and 42 s here.
- **The transform unit's cost adds up over its forms**, in instructions (form increments 888 G plus the dense form's 106 G against 982 G, 1.3%) and in peak memory (increments 5,035 MiB plus 964 MiB against 6,069 MiB, 1.2%). GCC at `-O0` holds the code of every form at once, so peak memory follows the number of forms in the unit. The four Gram forms of n = 4 and the n = 5 forms take 145 to 765 MiB each; the dense form about 800 MiB.
- Not measured: Clang's time trace of either unit; the variants on other presets; a unit after the restructuring plan 0043 proposes (its first step).
