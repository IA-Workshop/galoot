## Sampled rows

For each row: my own result first, then how it compares with the register.

### Medium rows (fresh inputs)

**A03, `GALOOT_CHECKING` mixed across units.** The first check used `antigrade`. I used integer division by zero instead. A checked unit and an unchecked unit each instantiate `V / int` over `int` in R(3,0,0), with the tag in a named namespace so both units share the entity, and the checked unit divides by 0.
- At -O0 on both compilers, linking the checked object first gives the library's abort ("an integer scalar cannot divide by zero", `linear.hpp:417`, exit 134).
- Linking the unchecked object first gives SIGFPE (exit 136): the checked unit ran the unchecked `checking_now()`.
- At -O2, both orders abort.

**Agree**: confirmed, medium, and the stated effect ("link order picks the definition; a checked unit runs unchecked") is right.

**A09, a name list without a final null.** Fresh inputs:
- `{'e','2',' ','e','1','3'}` builds silently, on both compilers, as two blades: e2, then e1.
- `char raw[8] = "e12"` (padded with nulls) is rejected with "blades: after the e, a name lists generator indices, each a digit from 1 to 9", which misnames the cause.
- `{'e','1','2',' ','e','3'}` loses the 3, and the bare "e" is rejected the same way.

So a list goes wrong silently only when its last name has two or more indices. Otherwise it is rejected with a misleading message. **Agree**: confirmed, medium. This could help H1 pin the rejected forms, but it is not a finding.

**A21, term-list capacity from 22 generators.** My prediction from `product_capacity` (`blade_product.hpp:186-192`) and `pair_capacity` (`plan.hpp:147-153`): Clang refuses a pair whose blades (for an antiproduct, whose complements) have a union of 22 or more generators, because its constant-evaluation array limit is 2^20. Results, on `R<n, 0, 0, 32>` over double, with masks written by hand:

| Operation | n = 21, Clang | n = 22, Clang | GCC | Fits the formula |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| e1 times the antiscalar (geometric product) | builds | refused, 3 errors | builds | yes |
| `bulk_dual(e1)` | builds | refused, 3 errors | builds | yes |
| `weight_dual(e1)` | builds | refused, 3 errors | builds | yes |
| `right_complement(e1)` | builds | builds | builds | yes |
| `e1 % e1` (complements' union 21) | builds | builds | builds | yes |
| antiscalar ⟇ antiscalar (complements empty) | builds | builds | builds | yes |

Antiscalar ⟇ antiscalar also builds at 24 and at 32, on both compilers. **Agree**: confirmed, medium, and the formula in the row is right. The prose says more than holds, as minor finding N1 explains.

**A22, the float16 floor.** My derivation from `padded_magnitude` (`transform.hpp:496-513`) and the tolerance (`:578-583`): each padded product contributes at least τ to P, and γ_(K+1) ≈ (K+1)u. So the least tolerance is about γ_(K+1)·W·τ = (K+1)·W·λ, which is (K+1)·W·2^-14 for binary16.

I evaluated `transform_dropped_tolerances` at v = x = 0 for the row's R(4,0,0) layouts (v on 1, e12, e13, e14; x on e1), over `std::float16_t` on GCC. Every dropped coefficient has K = 3 and a floor of 4.892e-4, which is 2.004 × (K+1)·2^-14, so W = 2. Over double the same ratio is exactly 2.000.

**Agree**: confirmed, medium. The row's corrected text ("about (K + 1) W 2^-14 … 4.9e-4") is right, so M1 is resolved.

**A36, narrow unsigned scalars.** I ran the two paths the first check did not rerun, under UBSan on both compilers, with run-time inputs.
- **The scalings.** `s * x` and `x * s` with s = 65535 over `std::uint16_t` report "signed integer overflow: 65535 * 65535 cannot be represented in type 'int'" at `linear.hpp:364`.
- **The scaled-term path.** I used the form diag(−200, 200), where e12 · e12 = +40000, a coefficient that `uint16_t` holds, so the library accepts it. (65535 e12)(1 e12) reports the same overflow at `mv.hpp:313` ("65535 * 40000"; GCC prints the operands the other way round).
- With g11 = g22 = 32767, the library refuses the coefficient at compile time (`product.hpp:291`), as designed.

**Agree**: confirmed, medium, on every site the row lists.

**A49, `rotation` about an l that is not a line.** My input was l with direction (0, 0, 1) and moment (0, 0, 1), so v · m = 1, θ = 1, `GALOOT_CHECKING=0`, on both compilers.

### Dismissed rows

**A12, a mask listed twice in one set.** `mv` refuses such a layout with "mv: the layout holds one blade twice" (`mv.hpp:577`; my probe hit it on both compilers). So a repeated mask reaches a plan only through `product_plan` on a hand-written set. There, each place gets its own terms (`plan.hpp:260-262`), and they sum on one result position, which is the correct linear result. **Agree**: dismissed.

**A15, shifts by the width at 32 generators.** I read every shift in the headers.
- `low_bits` shifts right by `mask_bits − n`, defined for n from 1 to 32.
- The one shift by N is guarded by `if constexpr (N < mask_bits)` (`blade_product.hpp:283-289`).
- Every left shift of `mask_t{1}` is by a generator index below N.
- The shifts of `std::size_t` or `std::uint64_t` are by at most 32, or are guarded (`transform.hpp:534`).

**Agree**: dismissed.

**A28, `get` of an absent blade.** The accepting `get` requires `has_name`, and the rejecting one requires its negation (`mv.hpp:1286-1338`). Every refused cause has its own `static_assert`, so the rejecting body's `x.coefficients[0]` is never compiled to run. **Agree**: dismissed.

**A44, the counts of roundings and the padding.** I read `kernel_roundings` (`transform.hpp:422-439`) against `accumulate` and `run_product` (`mv.hpp:298-369`).
- **The counts.** A product rounds once, a scaling by |c| ≠ 1 once more, and an addition after the larger of its two counts. That is the count of Higham's Lemma 3.3 along each path. The first term is assigned, and −value is exact.
- **The magnitudes.** The padded computation rounds twice per counted rounding (the operation, then the padding) and once per sum, so "at most 2K times on a path" (`:470-471`) is an upper bound.
- **Underflow.** The padding added after the scaling by |c| also covers that scaling's own underflow.

**Agree**: dismissed.

**A47, the exact divisions of the change of basis.**
- `eliminate_entries` divides only by the previous pivot, which is nonzero, because the elimination returns at a zero pivot (`basis_change.hpp:441-445`).
- The fraction-free Gauss-Jordan form keeps every entry a minor of [M | I], so each division is exact, and a row swap among rows at or below k keeps that.
- `inverts` (`:477-496`) then checks M·(pM⁻¹) = p·I, so an inexact division would be caught anyway.
- The gcd divisions have a positive argument (`typed_change`, `inverse_rows`), or are guarded against a divisor of 0 or 1 (`reduced`, `:377`).

**Agree**: dismissed.

## Two headers read end to end

**`unary.hpp`.** I checked each function and claim:
- right(right(u)) = (−1)^(gr·ag) u, because u ∧ right(u) = (−1)^(gr·ag) right(u) ∧ u.
- left(u) = (−1)^(gr·ag) right(u), and the two agree when n is odd, since one of gr and ag is then even.
- An unqualified `reverse` under `using namespace std::ranges` finds an object, which turns off argument-dependent lookup.
- A derived class binds through deduction to `const mv<Alg, S, T>&`.
- The negations in the maps run through `accumulate`: over `uint16_t`, −value is computed in `int` and converted back, which is defined, and over `int`, −min() is the scalar's own overflow, which A23 covers.

**No missing site.**

**`product.hpp`.**
- The wedge's and antiwedge's coefficients are ±1, as stated.
- The products' scalar checks (`coefficients_exact`) are A23's and A36's context.
- The capacity and compile-cost claims are A21's.

**One site the register lacks** (minor finding N4): the eight rejecting overloads (`product.hpp:252-258`, `275-280`, `305-310`, `322-327`, `369-375`, `397-402`, `429-434`, `448-453`). They require `mismatched_operands<X, Y>`, which a class derived from an `mv` does not meet. So a product of a derived class with an operand of another scalar type or algebra gets the compiler's own error. I reproduced it on both compilers:
- `D{…} * Vf{…}`, with D derived from an `mv` over double and Vf over float, gives "no match for 'operator*'" or "invalid operands", 1 error.
- `D{…} ^ W{…}`, with W in another algebra, gives the same.
- `V{…} * Vf{…}` gives the library's "a * b: the operands have different scalar types".

The file's head comment states this (`product.hpp:41-42`, "gets the compiler's own error when it is not"), but no declaration does. No test pins it: the `*_derived_fail` tests in `test/core/` cover conversions, `part`, `truncate` and the sums with a number, not products. It is the same class as A10 and A11.
