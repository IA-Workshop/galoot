## Blocking

### B1. The `-ffinite-math-only` option breaks O7's non-finite check without a compile error (S15)

`include/galoot/core/transform.hpp:104` stops compilation only when `__FAST_MATH__` is defined. The check's detection of NaN, infinity, and an overflowing magnitude (`transform.hpp:568-570`, and O7 as ruled) depends on IEEE 754 semantics for non-finite values. `-ffinite-math-only` relaxes those semantics, and the compiler reports it through `__FINITE_MATH_ONLY__`. S15 requires such a header to stop with `#error` "when the compiler reports such an option", and decision 0180 item 5 adopts S15 for this header, so the header complies only in part.

Failure scenario, reproduced: I built `test/core/transform_rounding_assert_fail.cpp` with `-std=c++23 -O2 -ffinite-math-only -DGALOOT_CHECKING=1` at `43355bb`.

| Compiler | Case | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| g++ 16 | `infinite` | exits 0 (should abort) |
| g++ 16 | `overflow` | exits 0 (should abort) |
| clang++ 23 | `nan` | exits 0 (should abort) |
| clang++ 23 | `infinite` | exits 0 (should abort) |

Every other case still aborts, and at -O0 every case aborts. The checking build compiles silently and passes non-finite dropped coefficients, which is the opposite of O7.

Suggested fix: also stop when `defined(__FINITE_MATH_ONLY__) && __FINITE_MATH_ONLY__ == 1` in a checking build, name the option in the message or the comment, and add a compile test beside `core.transform_fast_math`. Options that the compiler does not report, such as `-fassociative-math` alone, are outside S15's reach, and the comment could say so.

## Non-blocking (owner items first)

### N1 (owner, O6). Realistic use trips the check far more often than the characterization shows

The flat-plane firings are not a defect of the bound and not a margin problem. They are the limit that decision 0180 documents, but users will hit it routinely.

- **The bound is sound for these cases.** I recomputed 30 firing cases exactly in rationals: vectors moved by composed flat-plane motors, from my chain probe below at N = 16. In every case |e| ≈ |d|, up to 6.2 times the tolerance, and |d − e| ≤ 0.055 times the tolerance. The firing is V's own defect, e ≠ 0 on the antiscalar coefficient, and the transform's rounding plays no part.
- **Why rounded versors usually pass, and when they fail.** Each dropped term holds V twice, so a V with componentwise relative error ε gives |e| ≤ (2ε + ε²) M to first order. A V whose components are each correctly rounded therefore stays well inside γ_(K+1) M. I built exact rational versors from flat planes and general hyperplanes with non-dyadic coefficients and rounded each component once. The check never fired in 60,000 cases, largest ratio 0.30. The same versors composed by `compose` in double from rounded planes fired 24 times. Their components carried errors up to 6 × 10^4 u from cancellation. So the check fires when V was computed with cancellation or accumulated error, not merely because V was rounded.
- **Chains of rigid motions are the common case, and there the rate grows with the chain.** The characterization (`records/spikes/transform-rounding/`) composed only two or three planes, once. A program that composes rigid motions in conformal(3), such as a kinematic chain or an animation loop, gets these rates (1,000 trials per row, seed 20261107, each motor the composition of two random flat planes, renormalized by a power of two only):

| Motors composed (N) | 2 | 4 | 8 | 16 | 32 | 64 | 128 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| conformal(3) flat-plane motors, vector: share that fires | 0.2% | 0.4% | 2.2% | 3.6% | 6.0% | 11.4% | 22.7% |
| conformal(3) general hyperplanes: share that fires | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| rigid(3) control: share that fires | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |

  The general-hyperplane chains never fired, with a largest ratio of 0.48. Why the flat ones fire and the general ones do not is still unexplained here too.

Judgement: this is a limit to document, not a defect, and no fixed margin can cure it, since e grows with V's own error. It is still a false abort that conformal(3) users will meet in checking builds. When it fires, the only remedy is to turn checking off. Neither the comment of `transform` (`transform.hpp:664-686`) nor the comment at the check (`transform.hpp:558-562`) says so. The owner ruled O6 on the information "about one case in a thousand". With this data, I recommend the owner confirm or revise O6 before the `develop` merge. The options I see:

- (a) Keep the check, and state the cause and the remedy at the check and in the comment.
- (b) Check, for a rounding scalar, only the dropped parts that are not zero by a polynomial identity in V. That needs the compile-time zero test of plan 0001's D8 (M7). Today it would leave conformal(3) vectors unchecked on rounding scalars.
- (c) An allowance for V's defect. It cannot tell a slightly defective versor from a non-versor, so it weakens what the check is for.

### N2 (owner, item c). Checking mode's wall time comes mostly from subnormal microcode assists caused by the padding

The cost record attributes checking mode's cost, by inference, to the magnitude loop and the run-time division. The measured cause of most of the wall time is different.

- **Mechanism.** In `padded_magnitude` (`transform.hpp:496`), a padded magnitude of about λ meets an input |v_b| < 1 whenever a middle coefficient's products are all zero. Their product is then subnormal, and on this Intel CPU every subnormal result costs a microcode assist.
- **Measurement.** Pinned to CPU 4, an i9-14900K performance core, under the timing lock, with 10^7 iterations:

| Program | FP assists | Cycles | Instructions | Wall |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `records/spikes/transform-rounding/cost.cpp`, checking | 110,000,000 (11 per iteration) | 20.18 G | 24.47 G | 3.48 s |
| the same, unchecked | 0 | 0.54 G | 2.29 G | 0.22 s |
| my variant, the same zero-rich inputs, checking | 110,000,000 | 20.11 G | 24.21 G | 3.41 s |
| my variant, every coefficient nonzero, checking | 0 | 4.60 G | 24.21 G | 0.82 s |
| my variant, unchecked, either input | 0 | 0.29 G | 1.24 G | 0.09 s |

  About three quarters of the checking-mode cycles on the record's workload are assists. The record's "about 27 times its wall time" holds for inputs with zeros, which are common: motors without a translation, points with z = 0. It is not a figure for the check in general.
- **What I confirmed.** The record's instruction counts are exact: 24,472,626,319 and 2,292,621,387 on GCC, as reported. The unchecked object code of `cost.cpp` is identical at `01835b7` and `43355bb` on both compilers (objdump), so unchecked users pay nothing at run time.
- **Suggested fix, inferred and not measured here.** Pad with λ/u in place of λ. A larger padding only enlarges P, so Lemmas A to C still hold. Products of a padded value with an input of magnitude at least u, or zero, then stay normal. Also amend the cost README: name the assists as the main cause and say that the wall-time ratio depends on the inputs. No owner bound applies (C14), so this need not block.

### N3 (owner, item a). A padded magnitude that overflows fails the check: I agree

The magnitude only bounds the error while it is finite. Failing on overflow follows O7's principle that a checking build fails what it cannot verify. The window in which P̂ overflows while d and the kept coefficients stay finite needs |v|² |x| near 10^308. Decision 0180 records the choice with its options, and C18 lets it take effect when recorded. The owner may confirm it, but nothing waits on a ruling.

### N4 (owner, item b). `#error` under `-ffast-math` in a checking build: conformant to S15, and broad

The header's correctness depends on IEEE semantics, and the compiler reports the option, so S15 requires the `#error`. `core.hpp` includes `transform.hpp`, so a checking build under `-ffast-math` cannot use any of the core, even a program that never uses a rounding scalar. A narrower form would be a `static_assert` in the rounding-scalar branch of `transform`, which fires only when a float or double transform is instantiated. That is a reading of S15 that only the owner can accept, so I do not ask for it. The comment rightly says that a flush-to-zero mode set by other means, such as linking an object built with `-ffast-math` or loading a library that sets FTZ (knowledge-base note `compiler-floating-point-semantics.md`), is outside the model.

### N5. `std::bfloat16_t` no longer compiles with `transform`, even with checking off

libstdc++ gives `std::numeric_limits<std::bfloat16_t>` `is_iec559` true and `digits` 8, so `rounding_checked_scalar` (`transform.hpp:413`) admits it. For rigid(3), a point moved by a motor has K = 12, and (K + 1)(2K + 1)u = 325/256 > 1, so the `static_assert` at `transform.hpp:538` fails. The message is "transform: a dropped coefficient has too many roundings for the margin of the rounding check". That assert fires on every instantiation, with `GALOOT_CHECKING` 0 too, because constant evaluation always checks. I reproduced it: the same translation unit compiles against the headers of `01835b7` and fails at `43355bb`. `std::float16_t` passes: 0 of 200,000 random rigid(3) cases fired, largest ratio 0.309.

No present user and no claim in 4.5, so this does not block. The behavior is undocumented, though, and the message names the library's machinery. Suggested fix: state in the comment of `transform` that a rounding scalar too coarse for the check is rejected, or leave such scalars unchecked as for `std::complex<double>`, and record the choice in decision 0180.

### N6. The new message (usability checklist)

The message is "transform: v is not a versor on x within rounding" (`transform.hpp:570`), 49 characters.

- **Met:** it starts with the operation, names no internal machinery, and a failed constant evaluation shows it on the echoed line, as the tests show.
- **Not met: one message for four causes.** The causes are a non-versor, V's own rounding error (N1, the common real cause, for which "v is not a versor" is misleading), a NaN or infinite coefficient, and a magnitude that overflows. Plan R11's proposal had "or it carries rounding error of its own". The line budget does not hold it, as decision 0179 found for the exact message.
- **Not met: no fix after a semicolon.** Same line-budget reason.
- **Not met: not recorded in a decision.** Decision 0179 records WU13a's messages. Decision 0180 says only "a message of its own", and no decision gives the text (grep for "within rounding" under `docs/agent/decisions/`).
- **Suggested fixes.** Put the causes and the remedy in the comment at the check, which is where the abort's file and line point the user. Consider a distinct message for the non-finite and overflow cases. Append the text to decision 0179 or 0180.

- **Not listed as a departure:** criterion 3's "inputs scaled near 1e-300". The tests scale V by 2^-360 and x by 2^-340, about 10^-108 and 10^-103, so that second-stage products reach 2^-1060. That serves the criterion's intent better than inputs near 10^-300, whose products are all zero, but the outcome does not list it (C20).
- **Run-time cases cover double only.** `ten_times`, `tenth`, `nan`, `infinite`, and `overflow` run over double alone. Float gets `not_a_versor_float`, `underflow_float`, the constant evaluation, and the randomized test. This is a small gap.

### N8. Conservative but harmless

These are the mutants `coefficient_padding_removed` and `gamma_no_denominator` below.

- **The second padding.** The padding after the |c| product (`transform.hpp:500`) is not needed. With |c| ≥ 2 an integer, c times a computed product rounds only relatively: a subnormal result is exact, and a normal one has η = 0.
- **The denominator of γ.** Under the margin of one rounding, the denominator 1 − ku is also not needed. The hand case pins the documented formula, so dropping it is caught anyway.

Neither affects soundness, and keeping both makes the comment's derivation literal.

## Soundness of the check, derived independently (item 1)

- **By hand.** I rederived Lemmas A, B, and C of decision 0180, and they hold.
  - Lemma A is a straight induction on the DAG of the two kernels, with |η| ≤ λu ≤ γ_K λ absorbed by each product's padding.
  - Lemma B's lower bound, fl(fl(z) + λ) ≥ (1 − u)²(z + λ) via fl(z) ≥ (1 − u)z − λu, holds with subnormal intermediates too. The padded values themselves stay normal, as it says, but the intermediate products do not (N2).
  - Lemma C's algebra, 1 − (K + 1)(2K + 1)u(1 − Ku) > 0, is correct.
  - The final step, |d| ≤ fl(g P̂) by monotonicity because |d| is a value of T, is sound.
- **K counting matches `accumulate`.** The counting in `kernel_roundings` matches `accumulate` in `mv.hpp:299-320`: a product, then C times it when |C| ≠ 1; the first term assigned; negation exact.
- **The computation of g matches the derivation.** `transform.hpp:552` computes g as fl(ku / (1 − ku)), with ku and 1 − ku exact.
- **Simulation of the general claim.** I dumped the first and dropped kernels of 11 transforms from the core: rigid(3) motor and flector on point, line, and plane; conformal(3) plane, motor, and flector on a vector; a motor on a bivector; R(4,0,0) even V on a scalar and on a vector. I simulated them in Python: exact `Fraction` values for e, IEEE doubles for d, and the core's tolerance formula.
  - The simulation equals the core's d and tolerance bit for bit on a probe input for all 11 transforms.
  - I checked the general claim |d − e| ≤ tol for any input, not only versors, which Lemma A gives, over 6,600 random inputs with 53-bit significands. The six regimes were ordinary, mixed scales from 2^-400 to 2^300, deep underflow, underflow with 30% zeros, one huge coefficient with tiny others, and near overflow.
  - There were no violations. The largest |d − e| / tol was 0.287.
- **R9's additive bound is unsound, as decision 0180 says.** γ_K M + K · 2^-1074 failed in 5,219 comparisons in the same runs, by up to 9.0 × 10^14. The worst cases were rigid(3) motors on points, where e = 0 by identity, so they would be genuine false aborts. Decision 0180's own probe reported a factor of about 900. Mine is larger only because of the scaling chosen.
- **Contraction.** Decision 0180 asserts that contraction keeps the bound, and no test covers it, since the test build uses `-ffp-contract=off`. I sampled it once: the chain probe built at `-O2 -march=native -ffp-contract=fast` (391 FMA instructions) never fired for the rigid(3) control, largest ratio 0.28, or for general hyperplanes, largest 0.64. That is a sampled claim, not a proof.
- **Search for counterexamples.**
  - I found no true versor with correctly rounded input that fails, in 60,000 conformal(3) cases (N1).
  - In rigid(3) a false abort is impossible by identity, and I saw none.
  - A non-versor passing by a wide margin: in conformal(3) the dropped part of a non-versor is its exact e. It passes only when |e| is within about γ_(K+1) M, so a non-versor passes only when its defect is at rounding level relative to M. No case passed "by a wide margin".

## Tests: my mutants (item 2)

I ran fifteen mutants of `include/galoot/core/transform.hpp`, distinct from the implementer's seventeen, each on gcc-debug and clang-debug. Each mutant was applied alone. I built `core_transform_rounding_test`, `core_transform_rounding_mode_1`, `core_transform_rounding_mode_0`, and `core_transform_test` with `ninja -k 0`, ran `ctest -L core.transform`, and restored the header. After a failed build, the restore step rebuilt the targets before the next mutant.

A kill is a failing test, or a build failure that is a test's own `static_assert` or a constant evaluation that a test asks for. A build failure leaves an older binary in place, and that binary's test results are ignored.

### N9. Two test gaps the mutants expose

- **`size_signed`.** No abort case has a negative dropped coefficient, so a check that drops the absolute value passes every test. In R(4,0,0), V = a + b e1234 on x = 1 gives V ⟇ x ⟇ V̰ = (a² + b²) + 2ab e1234, by the products that `transform_rounding_cases.hpp:74-77` states. So V = 1 − e1234 drops −2, which I confirmed by running the kernels. Adding that case to `transform_rounding_assert_fail.cpp`, and to a constant-evaluation test, kills the mutant.
- **`middle_magnitude_restricted`.** The rounding tests never meet a layout where the dropped terms read a middle blade that the kept terms do not. WU13a pinned such a layout for the values: the degenerate form of `f57800a`, with V on e1, e2, e3, e123 and x on e123. If the magnitude used the restricted first stage there, the tolerance would miss terms and could fall below |d| for a versor, which is a false abort. Suggested: a hand case or a family in such a form, with exact versors, requiring tol ≥ γ_K M.
- **`concept_not_exact`.** This would be killed by a `static_assert` on a scalar whose `numeric_limits` is specialized as inexact but not IEEE.
