## Task 2: what `transform` gives, derived first

### Derivation (mine)

In R(n, 0, 0) write I = 𝟙 = e12…n. From 3.1, right(x) = x̃ I and left(x) = I x̃ on basis blades (u ũ = 1, and ũ I is a multiple of the complementary blade), and so for all x by linearity. With 4.2:

    a ⟇ b = I (ã I b̃ I)~ = I Ĩ b Ĩ a = b Ĩ a,  since I Ĩ = 1.

For n = 3, Ĩ = −I and I is central, so a ⟇ b = −I b a: the antiproduct reverses the order of its factors. Applying this twice, V ⟇ x ⟇ W = −W x V. In n = 3 the antireverse is minus the reverse, by the signs of 2.1 at each grade, so V ⟇ x ⟇ V̰ = Ṽ x V.

A classical rotor r = s + b12 e12 + b13 e13 + b23 e23 has antigrades 3 and 1, so it is odd under 4.4. With n odd, the odd form is V ⟇ x̂ ⟇ V̰. Therefore:

    transform(x, r) = r̃ x̂ r = (−1)^k r̃ x r   for x of grade k.

On a vector this is −r̃ v r = −λ R⁻¹(v), where R is the rotation of the classical sandwich r v r̃ = λ R(v) and λ = r r̃. It is the opposite turn, negated. It agrees with r v r̃ only where R² v = −v. In R(3, 0, 0) that happens only for a quarter turn and a vector in the rotor's plane. The plan's probe (r = 1 − e12, v = e1) was that one case.

The classical action is recovered with a complement. In n = 3, right(r) = r̃ I is even under 4.4, and transform(x, r̃ I) = (−I r) x (r̃ I) = r x r̃.

In the rga3 preset, a motor has antigrades 2, 0, 2 and 4, so it is even and transform(x, Q) = Q ⟇ x ⟇ Q̰. Under 5.5, the motor l sin φ + 𝟙 cos φ turns by 2φ counterclockwise about the unitized line l.

### Exact check

The check is `check_transform.py`, on my own `ga.py`: an algebra over a diagonal metric written from CONVENTIONS 1 to 4 for this check, with blades as bitmasks and coefficients as `Fraction` or SymPy symbols. It shares no code with the library, with `test/reference/`, or with the author's scripts. 79 checks pass, including these:

- The definitions match the specification: u ∧ right(u) = 𝟙 and left(u) ∧ u = 𝟙 on all 16 blades of rigid(3), right(e1) = e423, and 𝟙 is the identity of the antiproduct.
- With symbolic coefficients for a general rotor and for x of every grade, in R(n, 0, 0) for n = 2, 3, 4 and 5: transform(x, r) = (−1)^k r̃ x r, and transform(x, right(r)) = transform(x, left(r)) = r x r̃. On vectors, transform(v, r) differs from r v r̃ as polynomials in every one of these n.
- For r = b a = 1 − e12 (a = e1, b = e1 + e2):

  | v | r v r̃ | transform(v, r) |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | e1 | 2 e2 | 2 e2 |
  | e2 | −2 e1 | −2 e1 |
  | e3 | 2 e3 | −2 e3 |
  | (1, 2, 3) | (−4, 2, 6) | (−4, 2, −6) |

  The raw sandwich r ⟇ v ⟇ r̰ is the negation of `transform` on vectors in this odd case.
- In rga3:
  - e43 + 𝟙 sends (1, 0, 0, 1) to 2 (0, 1, 0, 1), with λ = 2.
  - The unitized motor (3/5) L + (4/5) 𝟙, with L the line through (1, 2, 0) along z, sends (2, 2, 5, 1) to (1 + 7/25, 2 + 24/25, 5, 1). That is a counterclockwise turn by 2φ, where cos 2φ = 7/25.
  - The translation by (1, 3, −2) moves the origin to (1, 3, −2, 1).
  - The order of `compose` holds, and so does the transform of a line as the join of the moved points.

## Findings

1. **Important (a gap in the order, not a wrong value): "blade" is used before it is defined.** K204 ("a blade's name lists its generators") and K207 ("the canonical layout of the blades it can reach") are both in chapter 2 above K210, which defines "blade". The inventory's own rule is that a row may use only rows above it, and `tests.docs_terms` will hold the pages to this order. The author's script checks IDs only, so it cannot see this.
   - Failure: a glossary and a chapter 2 built from the record as it stands use the term two rows before its defining instance.
   - Fix (any one): move K210, or a first form of it ("a basis blade is a wedge of distinct generators"), above K204; or reword K204 and K207 without the word and let K210 introduce it.
   - Recommendation: the first form above K204, with K210 keeping grade, scalar and trivector.
2. **Minor: K702 says that with rigid(3) "the three counts of `R<P, Q, Z>` now all have a meaning".** No tutorial row introduces a generator that squares to −1. The notation key's R(p, q, r) row ("in full in chapter 7") says the same. The Q count is defined only in X03, outside the tutorial.
   - Failure: a chapter 7 written to the row asserts a meaning it never teaches.
   - Fix: add "a generator may also square to −1" to K702's text, or say that the middle count stays zero in the tutorial.
3. **Minor (wording): K307 and M24 say that `truncate` "checks that what it drops is zero" without the condition.** By CONVENTIONS 4.5 and `mv.hpp` (lines 1035 to 1039), it checks only for an exact scalar, and then in a constant evaluation or with `GALOOT_CHECKING` 1.
   - Failure: over `double`, a reader believes a check that never runs.
   - The tutorial's `static_assert`s over `int` are covered, and K905 adds checking mode later, so the fix is a clause: "over an exact scalar, in a constant expression".
4. **Minor (wording): K606 describes the odd form as "the same with the sign that makes it the reflection".** The odd form applies the grade involution to x, so the sign depends on x's grade. It is not one sign.
   - Failure: right for the vectors chapter 6 transforms. Wrong if a page applies the sentence to a bivector of R(3, 0, 0), where the sign flips.
   - Fix: say "for a vector" in the row, or name the per-grade sign.
5. **Minor: two records pull against each other on quarter turns.** The learner contract of chapter 10 says the reader can "check the model exactly with quarter turns". Authoring rule A2 says a test of a rotation uses an angle that is not a multiple of a quarter turn.
   - For motors in rigid(3) there is no quarter-turn coincidence of the M31 kind, so the contract is not wrong.
   - Recommendation: add the exact 3-4-5 turn of A2 (2 − e12, complemented) to chapter 10's exact check, so that the arm's check could tell a turn from its opposite.
