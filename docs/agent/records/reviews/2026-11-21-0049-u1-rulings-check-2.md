### Finding 5 (minor, confirmed): "where every generator squares to 1 or −1" names the wrong criterion

M15 (`misconceptions.md:51`) says the bulk-dual form holds "in any R(p, q, 0), where every generator squares to 1 or −1, and not in general in a form with other squares". The card row (`:94`) has the same gloss and names "a form with other squares" as the failing case. R(p, q, 0) is diagonal by CONVENTIONS 1.2, so the statement is true.

Failure: a reader who takes the gloss as the condition is misled both ways. In `from_gram({{1, 1, 0}, {1, -1, 0}, {0, 0, 1}})`, every generator squares to 1 or −1, and the bulk-dual form fails on every versor tried (`sandwich_scope.py`, and `scope_probe.cpp` against the library). In conformal(3), two generators square to 0, and it holds on every versor tried.

Fix: "in R(p, q, 0), a diagonal form whose squares are 1 or −1", and "not in general in another form, such as one with a square of 2".

### Finding 6 (minor, plausible): the proved scope is wider than "R(p, q, 0)", and conformal(d) follows from CONVENTIONS 1.8

The card's "Must not" (`:94`) bars the bulk-dual form "for conformal(d) or any other form outside R(p, q, 0), for which nothing is proved", and its "Must say" says Theorem B "is proved for those forms only". Theorem B (note 0029 section 2.4) is stated for "a nondegenerate diagonal form with squares ±1", in any order. So diag(−1, 1, 1), which `from_gram` builds, is proved, although it is not R(p, q, 0) in CONVENTIONS 1.2's order. It holds there in both my tools.

For conformal(d), the proof is short (Inferred; I have not checked the factors' direction against note 0026). CONVENTIONS 1.8 says a change of basis φ with determinant d gives φ(u★) = d φ(u)★ for any change. It preserves the geometric product and the reverse. When P⁻ᵀ G′ P⁻¹ = G, as for the change of 1.4, it preserves transform with the factor 1/d². Then φ(transform(x, V★)) = transform(φx, d (φV)★) / d² = transform(φx, (φV)★), and Theorem B in R(d+1, 1, 0) makes that C_{φV}(φx) = φ(C_V(x)). φ is invertible, so the statement holds in conformal(d). The same argument covers every nondegenerate form whose Gram matrix is its own inverse and has a real orthonormal change to a ±1 form. That matches section 1.2: the self-inverse forms hold, and every other form tried fails.

This is not an error. The records claim less than is true, which rule A3 permits. Fix (optional): write "outside the diagonal ±1 forms of Theorem B" for "outside R(p, q, 0)". Then either keep the bar on conformal(d), or have the note's author add the argument above as a corollary, checked as the theorems were, and lift the bar. See open item 1.
