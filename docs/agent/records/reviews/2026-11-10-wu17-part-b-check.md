## Discrepancies, ranked

1. **Medium: the assembly cross-check is not backed.** README, "Counts": "the point's 25 + 11 additions and subtractions equal the 15 + 21 of the probe's GCC assembly", and "Checks": "the point's additions and subtractions equal the assembly's". No script computes it, and the recorded `asm.gcc.s` does not give it: `transform_point_motor` has 15 `addsd`, 6 `addpd`, 7 `subsd`, 5 `subpd` and 1 `xorpd`, which is 33 add or subtract instructions, or 44 lanes if both lanes of each packed one are used. Neither is 36, and no reading I found gives a split of 15 and 21. The multiplications do agree (32 `mulsd` + 8 `mulpd` = 48 lanes). That file was also compiled at WU13's revision. The claim should be withdrawn or traced. Part E1 is where additions get read properly at the closing revision. The pass reported in "Checks" has not been shown, so under C20 it must not be reported as passing.
2. **Low to medium: B's per-call advantage is in multiplications only, and it is narrow.** Counting every arithmetic operation (mul + add + scl), B per call is 79 against A's 84 for the point and 130 against 130 for the line. B also does more additions than A for the line (62 against 58). B's additions are an upper bound, as the author says: computing the squared terms Hadamard-style (s1 = a + b, s2 = c + d, d1 = a − b, d2 = c − d for the squares of Q_vw, Q_vx, Q_vy, Q_vz) gives λ and the three diagonals in 8 additions instead of 12. That brings the point and the plane to 32 additions and the line to 61 (hand count, not run). Note 0027 should state the per-call comparison in all three units, so that "B beats A" is not read as a time prediction.
3. **Low: the batch comparison leaves out B against C.** The author's own figures show B per object (13 MA for the point and the plane, 27 for the line) beating C as written per object (22, 18, 36 with the motor doubled first). Counting MA + scl, B overtakes C from about 5 points, 6 planes, and 7 lines under one motor (point 48 + 13k < 4 + 22k for k ≥ 5; plane 48 + 13k < 19 + 18k for k ≥ 6; line 81 + 27k < 19 + 36k for k ≥ 7). B is exact and needs no assumption (C8), so this bears on D8. A matrix built under the constraints (B's matrix with λ = 1, which makes the weight entry 1) would be cheaper per object still. It is not among the author's forms and is not counted here.
4. **Low: "differs off the constraints" understates C's failure mode.** Scaling the motor only scales the sandwich (forms A and B): a point keeps its place and its weight becomes λw. Form C distorts it to first order. For the rotor (0, 0, 3/5, 4/5) scaled by (1 + e), acting on (1, 0, 0, 1), the unitized sandwich is (7/25, 24/25, 0, 1) for every e, and C gives (7/25 − 36e/25, 24/25 + 48e/25, 0, 1) (`drift.py`). Part F's bounds on drifted motors, and the assumption C8 makes C name, should start from this.
5. **Low: two of the author's caveats can be closed.** (a) "Where wedge and antiwedge write into a declared layout, the orientation's sign is folded into the terms ... assumed (not read) for products": `mv.hpp`'s `make_product_kernel` does fold it (`Target.blades[at].sign * t.coefficient`), so the assumption is confirmed by reading. (b) "the neg counts only by the same reading": the a-then-b order the neg counts rest on is enforced by `plan.hpp`'s `defects_of`, so they follow from a checked invariant, and my emulation, written separately, gives the same negs.
6. **Low, presentation.** (a) The "declared type" row of point ^ point ^ point keeps the intermediate canonical and writes only the final result onto the Plane layout. With the intermediate on the Line layout the counts are 8 add, 6 sub, 6 neg. The table should say which one it counts. (b) The "monomials per output" column of compose's canonical row repeats the declared order [4, 4, 4, 4, 8, 8, 8, 8]; in canonical order (1, e12, e13, e14, e23, e24, e34, e1234) it is [8, 8, 8, 4, 8, 4, 4, 4]. (c) "Form C for compose is not defined: a unit constraint does not remove a term from a bilinear form" is a plausible argument but not a proof. It should be marked inferred.
7. **Low: CONVENTIONS 5.8 against the errata text.** The author's reading is right. The errata copy says that in Equation (3.219) of the fifth and earlier printings "Q_vw should be the coefficient of the scalar term 1, and Q_mw ... of the antiscalar term 𝟙". CONVENTIONS 5.8, the preset's `motor_layout` (e41 e42 e43 e1234 e23 e31 e12 1, with Q_vw on 𝟙 and Q_mw on 1), and the wiki's Motor page as quoted in note 0023 (M1) all say the opposite. Two qualifications. First, "no count or polynomial here depends on which" holds for the counts and for polynomials written in blade coefficients, and h is symmetric in the two letters, but λ is not: under the errata's naming it would read |v|² + Q_mw², so the letter forms in the README (s = Q_vw) would need renaming. Second, this disagrees between two Lengyel sources, the book's Eq. (3.219) as corrected and the wiki's Motor page. C11 asks for such a disagreement to be recorded in section 8. The wiki ranks first and 5.8 needs no change, but whether to add a row is the owner's call (item O-b below).
8. **Information for part C.** (a) Whether `transform` takes its checking path at run time depends on the counting scalar. It does when the scalar is `exact_scalar` (`numeric_limits<T>::is_exact`) and equality-comparable and GALOOT_CHECKING is 1. Without a `numeric_limits` specialization `is_exact` is false, the path is never taken, and the counts are 48/72/48 in every preset. (b) Every kernel call default-constructs one T{} for each place of its result (`zero_places`), and `mv`'s own default construction adds more. The counting rule leaves these out ("constructing a zero"), while plan 0038 item 3 counts "constructions from an integer". Part C has to state whether T{} is one of them.

## The author's four open questions

1. **The MA unit. Agree** that MA serves only to compare with published counts, that every arrangement should be reported (48 to 63 for the line and 30 to 39 for the plane under the author's rule, and 57 and 36 under mine for the arrangement with the factor 2 on the results), and that mul, add, neg and scl stay the primary units. Tradeoff: one number for 0027 would read more simply, but it would hide a spread of 6 to 9 MA, which is as large as the gap to the published figures. Recommendation: report the comparison with Lengyel as "not reproduced; formula and rule differ; book unread", never as an erratum.
2. **Checking-mode counts in the tests. Agree** that they belong in the tests, as long as the counting scalar declares itself exact (finding 8a). Otherwise they cannot be observed at run time. Options: (a) an exact counting scalar, with both sets of expected counts chosen by GALOOT_CHECKING; (b) a non-exact scalar, giving one set of counts in every preset and leaving the checking path's cost untested. Tradeoff: (a) costs two expected tables and preset-dependent expectations; (b) is simpler and blind to an extra term in the dropped-blade kernel. Recommendation: (a), because the ON presets exist and the dropped-blade kernel is code that a mutant can change.
3. **Pinning the neg counts. Agree.** They come from the source-level accumulate rule and from a plan order that `defects_of` enforces (finding 5b). A counting scalar sees source operations, not the compiler's, so the counts are deterministic. Tradeoff: any future change of term order, or collected plans, changes them and needs a W9 update of expected values. Recommendation: pin them, and state in the test that they follow from the plan order.
4. **Framing B as a batch feature. Agree, and the numbers make it stronger.** Per call, B wins only in multiplications: it ties or loses in total operations and loses in MA (finding 2). In a batch it wins from 2 objects, and it beats the constraint form C as written from about 5 to 7 objects under one motor (finding 3), with no assumption. Recommendation: note 0027 presents B as an optional exact "matrix of a motor, applied to many" interface beside transform, not as a replacement for transform's kernels. D8's per-call question then stays with form A.
