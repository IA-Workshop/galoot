## Findings

**Blocking (documentation only)**
- F1. The stated merge bound holds only for a canonical set merged with itself. The comment says Clang merges two sets of 128 blades (`full<7>`) but not of 256 and GCC two sets of 512 but not 1,024. Measured on the new header at default limits: Clang fails on its step limit in `unite` when merging two disjoint sets of 128 (the even-grade and odd-grade halves of `full<8>`) in either order, while 64 + 64 disjoint passes and a reversed `full<7>` with itself passes; GCC fails on the operation limit for disjoint 512 + 512 in both orders and for a reversed `full<9>` merged with itself, while canonical `full<9>` with itself passes and 256 + 256 disjoint passes in both orders. State what was measured ("merging `full<7>` with itself") and the lower figures, or state the bound in the size of the result; add the same to the 0090 note.

**Non-blocking**
- F2. Two test gaps shown by mutants that survive on both presets: (a) no test repeats, in the second input, a mask the first input lacks (under `u.in_second[at] = false`, `merge<{+e1},{+e2,+e2}>` compiles silently and `merge<{},{+e2,−e2}>` reports only the false "opposite signs"); (b) no test has a bad sign in the second input on a mask the first input holds (under dropping `is_sign(blade.sign)` at line 286, `merge<{+e1},{e1 sign 0}>` prints the false "opposite signs"). Add `merge_repeated_in_second_only` and `merge_bad_sign_second_shared`, each with FORBID "opposite signs".
- F3. The `signs_valid` comment ("adds almost nothing to a lookup") overstates on GCC: 38.96 G against 33.62 G at n = 10 is +16%, and +2% on Clang. Decision 0093 says so; the header comment does not.
- F4. The random test's comment claims "a failure names its case"; a wrong result gives only a total mismatch count and the seed (a rejected merge or compile error names `random_case<C>`), and the seed is printed in decimal while the source writes it in hex.
- F5. The random test killed no mutant that another test does not also kill, and costs +11.8 G, +0.8 s, and 2× peak memory on GCC, +24.8 G and +1.3 s on Clang in every preset that builds the file; its value is breadth (n = 9, 12, 32 with arbitrary masks and orders). Opinion: keep it at about 30 cases or move it into its own translation unit so it compiles in parallel; the owner's call.
- F6 (plausible, not confirmed). The SplitMix64 citation: no research note records that anyone read the paper (V9); the constants and shifts 30, 27, 31 are, from memory, Stafford's "Mix13" as used in Vigna's `splitmix64.c` and JDK 8's `SplittableRandom`, whereas the paper's `mix64` uses the MurmurHash3 finalizer with shifts of 33. Cite the source followed or drop the citation; the hash's quality does not matter here.
- F7 (style). Duplicated test helpers: `random_list` duplicates `mask_list`, and the comparison loop and `random_index_mismatches` duplicate `mismatches()` and `index_mismatches`; reuse them or say why not.
- F8 (pre-existing). `position_of`, `unite`, and `binomial` lack `[[nodiscard]]`.
