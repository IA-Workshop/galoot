## Corrections after the independent check, 2026-11-21

Written by: Claude (agent, Opus 5.5, math-expert and hci-expert roles), correcting the design records after the independent check of U1 (`docs/agent/records/reviews/2026-11-21-0049-u1-check.md` on `review/docs-design`, checked at `5175316`). The check found no error of substance and reported five findings. Each was judged against CONVENTIONS, the headers under `include/galoot/`, and the records. All five are confirmed and corrected; none is rejected.

**F1, "blade" used above the row that defines it: confirmed and corrected.** K204 ("a blade's name lists its generators") and K207 ("the blades it can reach") are in chapter 2 above K210, the first row that defined "blade". K204 now gives the first form, "Blade, first form: a wedge of distinct generators, also called a basis blade, such as e1 and the basis bivectors e12, e13, e23", and K210 is "Blade, refined: a wedge of any vectors, not only of generators", resting on K204 as well. This is the check's option (a) with no new ID, so no other record's reference to a K row changes. The first form also defines "basis blade", which K503 uses and no row defined.

Could `inventory_check.py` see this kind of gap? Not as it was: it read only the ID columns. It can see it for the term a row names first. In a row of kind term or symbol, the text before the first colon of the concept is the row's headword, and the inventory already writes defining rows that way ("Generator:", "Blade:", "Motor:") and marks first forms and refinements there. The script now takes the first row with a headword as its definition and fails if the concept text of a K row above it uses the headword or its plural as a whole word, outside backticks. A headword that a prerequisite row also uses (line, plane, from P08) has its school meaning above the tutorial's row and is not checked. The rule is mechanical and reads only the inventory's own columns; its limit is that a term named later in a cell ("grade" after "Blade: ...;") is not seen, and the script lists the term rows it could read no headword from (15 now, among them K106, K702 and K810). On the inventory as the check found it, the extended script reports K204 and K207, the gap of F1, and two more of the same kind, which are corrected here too:

- K101 defined "generator" as a direction "that an algebra is built from", above K102, which defines "algebra". It now reads "from which everything in the tutorial is built".
- K701 named "weight" as the fourth coordinate w at the end of its cell, and K713 defined "Bulk and weight" as a part of a multivector without saying that it changes the meaning. K701 is now "Weight, first form: a fourth coordinate w", and K713 is "Bulk and weight, refined: ... so that a point's weight is now w e4 and not the number w". The two meanings were already there; the rows now say which comes first.

The script also caught a forward use in my own first wording of F3's correction ("checking mode", defined in K905), which was reworded before the commit. A hand scan of thirty other terms for their first use, outside the script, found one more: K812 said "null generator", a word the tutorial never defines (X03 does, outside it); it now says "the generator that squares to zero". The inventory's opening paragraph and its section 4 say what the extended script checks.

**F2, K702 claims that all three counts of `R<P, Q, Z>` have a meaning: confirmed and corrected.** No tutorial row has a generator that squares to −1, so the middle count was never given a meaning. K702 now says what each count counts and that the tutorial's Q is always 0, which is all the tutorial needs and does not add a concept. K103 said "the other two counts are zero until chapter 7", which implied that both change there; it now says that chapter 7 says what they count. The notation key's R(p, q, r) row ("in full in chapter 7") is true of the corrected K702 and is unchanged.

**F3, `truncate`'s check stated without its condition: confirmed and corrected.** CONVENTIONS 4.5 and the comment on `truncate` in `include/galoot/core/mv.hpp` say that it checks what it drops only for an exact scalar, in every constant evaluation, and at run time only when `GALOOT_CHECKING` is 1; over a rounding scalar it checks nothing. K307 now says "over an exact scalar such as `int`, checks that what it drops is zero in a constant expression; chapter 9 adds the same check at run time" (K905 teaches checking mode). M24's correction says the same, and the chapter 3 contract in `learner-contracts.md`, which had the same unconditioned clause, now says "in whole numbers at compile time".

**F4, K606's "the sign that makes it the reflection": confirmed and corrected.** By CONVENTIONS 4.4 an odd V gives −V ⟇ x̌ ⟇ antireverse(V), where x̌ changes the sign of the blades of odd antigrade. The sign is therefore set by the antigrade of each part of x: −1 where the antigrade is even and +1 where it is odd, so a vector of R(3, 0, 0) (antigrade 2) and a bivector there (antigrade 1) take opposite signs, and so do a line and a point of rigid(3). One sign for every x, as the row read, is wrong for the parts of x whose antigrade has the other parity; chapter 6 transforms only vectors, so the row held there. K606 now states the rule with the vector case as its example and rests on K502 (antigrade) as well. Checked with a scratch probe of eight `static_assert`s over `int` (a mirror `!a` of R(3, 0, 0) on a vector and a bivector, and a flector of rigid(3) on a point and a line, each asserting the sign and that the other sign fails), compiled `-fsyntax-only` with GCC 16.2.1 and Clang 23.1.5 at `-std=c++23 -Wall -Wextra`, in 0.30 s and 0.40 s, outside the job pool as a small probe; it is not committed.

**F5, chapter 10's quarter turns against rule A2: confirmed and corrected.** Rule A2 says that a test of a rotation uses an angle that is not a multiple of a quarter turn, and the chapter 10 contract said the reader can check the arm's model exactly "with quarter turns". The contract is not wrong for motors, as the check says, but an author who follows both records cannot. The contract now keeps quarter turns, whose results the reader can find by hand, and adds a turn that is not a multiple of a quarter turn, the 3-4-5 turn of A2, so that no coincidence of quarter turns can pass. A2 is unchanged.
