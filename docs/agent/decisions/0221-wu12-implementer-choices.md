## Decision

1. **The form of the arithmetic (decision 0222 item 7).** Each result coefficient is y_B = (Σ c_AB x_A) / q_B. The integer sum is the existing straight-line kernel, `run_map` over `kernel_term`s, through `accumulate`: the terms in kernel order (by the input's position in its layout, then by the output blade's canonical order within one image), the first term of an output assigned, a coefficient of +1 or −1 an addition or a subtraction, any other `static_cast<T>(c) * x`. Then each output with q_B ≠ 1 is divided once, `out = static_cast<T>(out / static_cast<T>(q_B))`, by the scalar's own division: never a product with a reciprocal and never a division per term. q_B is the least denominator over which every term of y_B is an integer: the least common multiple of the products of row denominators of the inputs that reach B, with q_B and its c then divided by their gcd. That is the same as the least common multiple of the reduced denominators of the terms (if a prime p divided q_B and every c, the term whose reduced denominator has the highest power of p would have a numerator divisible by p, a contradiction), so it is the q_B of part N's record.
2. **The tolerance rule, checked against that form.** Part N's record assumed exactly this form ("The computation": the sum by `accumulate` in the kernel's order, then one division by `static_cast<T>(q_B)` when q_B ≠ 1, q_B the least common multiple of the reduced denominators). It names three forms that would void regime E's claims: a multiplication by a reciprocal, a division applied to each term, and a denominator that is not reduced. None is used. Regime C's counts follow from `tracked` running the same templates, as the record says. So the rule stands unchanged, and no tolerance moves. Observed (V10, revision `2e10061`, the default seed): every floating case of `basis_change_scalars_test.cpp` passes on gcc-debug and clang-debug; the largest ratios of error to bound in regime C are 0.473 (random 7), 0.708 (random 7 back), 0.683 (random 2), 0.499 (conformal(3)), and 0 (conformal(3) back, which has no coefficient of two or more roundings), the same on both compilers and the same as the test author's stand-in reported.
3. **The division is a fold with q_B a constant of the code.** `run_change` divides through `divide_by<q_B>` for each result position, a template whose body is empty when q_B = 1, so the code has no run-time branch or loop on the denominators (P4), and the conversion of q_B to the scalar is of a constant. A run-time loop over the denominators, as the test author's stand-in had, made GCC warn under `-Wconversion` that converting the run-time `std::int64_t` to `std::complex<double>`'s `double` may change its value; a constant that the scalar holds exactly is not warned about.
4. **The image of a blade composes the wedge's pair rule (C4, decision 0220 section 4.1).** `detail::blade_image<Capacity>(rows, mask)` folds the rows of the blade's generators one at a time, each step calling `pair_terms<BinaryOperation::wedge, 1>` with a default-constructed metric, which the wedge does not read (its documented guarantee), and multiplying by the row's entry with checked arithmetic. It returns the undivided integer minors, as `basis_change_identities_test.cpp` expects, and has two overloads, for a `basis_change<N>` as written and for the widened rows of the change or its inverse. A layout's terms are generated as `generate_plan` generates a plan: count, then fill, then `collected` and `type_terms`, so each image is folded twice per layout; images are not shared between blades. The measured limits (header comment) are well past every layout the tests use, so no memoized form was written (C3: the direct form first).
5. **The checks and the inverse.** The per-tag checks are one consteval function, `detail::make_checked_change<C>`, held in `detail::checked_change<C>`, in the order of decision 0220 section 3.4, each fault stopping the checks after it. Independence and the inverse come from one fraction-free Gauss–Jordan elimination of [M | I] (the Bareiss step a_ij ← (a_kk a_ij − a_ik a_kj)/p applied to every row but the pivot's, with a row swap for a zero pivot), which ends at [p I | p M⁻¹]. The inverse's rows are then p M⁻¹ times the denominators, over |p|, each row reduced by its gcd; the change's own rows are not reduced, since the per-output reduction of item 1 makes the terms the same either way. The isometry check forms M G′ once, so it costs 2n³ multiplications rather than n⁴. `detail::inverse_change(change)` is the same elimination callable at run time, as the identity test needs; it asserts independence with the text of the independence message.
6. **Two self-checks of the generated data (V4).** The elimination's right block times M must be p I (`basis_change: the computed inverse does not invert the rows`), and every term must keep its input blade's grade (`basis change map: a term has another grade than its input blade`). No user input reaches either while the generator is correct, so both go to `test/internal_messages.txt` with that reason, as the product plan's and the unary map's self-checks do. The independence check needs no self-check: the elimination's pivot is det M up to sign, and the identity test checks the antiscalar's image against a determinant it computes itself.
7. **The overflow message is kept on one source line.** A compiler that rejects a constant evaluation through `GALOOT_ASSERT` quotes the source line of the assertion, so a message split over several lines by the formatter shows only its first line, and `core.basis_change_overflow` could not find it. The one `GALOOT_ASSERT` of that message is in `detail::check_fits`, between `// clang-format off` and `// clang-format on`, with a comment that says why; `change_multiply` and `change_add` call it. This is the only place the core turns the formatter off.
   - Items 5 to 7, where they describe the checks of a tag, are superseded on 2026-11-09 by decision 0224: the checks' arithmetic notes an overflow instead of stopping, and one verdict feeds one static_assert per fault.
8. **`divides_exactly` reads `integer_limits`, not `numeric_limits<T>::is_integer`.** The test rational's specialization of `std::numeric_limits` defines `is_specialized` and `is_exact` only, so a constraint that names `is_integer` fails to substitute and makes the concept false for it; `integer_limits` treats a missing member as not an integer, as `holds_exactly` already does. Its accepting and rejecting `static_assert`s (`double`, `int`) are in the header, beside the concept (S7).
9. **Registrations and labels.** Part B registered every program and compile-fail test, with the labels plan 0030 named (`core.basis_change`, `core.basis_change_identities`, `support.diagonalize`); none is `slow` (the programs run in under 0.4 s each on gcc-debug). `tests.messages_pinned` gains the label `rga3.headers`, as decision 0219 item 13 asked of the next unit that touches `test/CMakeLists.txt` (`73eb2ac`). The new header joins `galoot_core`'s header set in the root `CMakeLists.txt`, which the configure step requires.
10. **One test added by the implementer, in its own commit (W9).** "the header's example: a conformal(3) point to R(4, 1, 0) and back" (`404f2fc`) runs the example of the header's comment, as decision 0220's checklist asked of part C.
11. **The trace row and CONVENTIONS.** The row of 1.4 "the change of basis sends e_A to the sum of det P[A, B] e'_B" is set to implemented (`1bac5a5`); no CONVENTIONS text is edited. When the owner ratifies 1.17, that row moves to 1.8, and 1.8 can take facts that the tests already state, such as "the change of basis keeps the dot, the reverse, the antireverse, and the grade involution" and "the bulk, the weight, and the complements depend on the basis"; they need markers that state them, written then.

## Options weighed

- For item 1, a stored reciprocal: one multiplication per output is cheaper than a division, but it rounds twice and needs the reciprocal in the scalar; ruled out by decision 0222 item 7.
- For item 3, a run-time loop with the warning suppressed by a pragma (the stand-in's form): a suppression where the fold needs none, and a branch per output at run time.
- For item 4, the wedge rule written inline with `reorder_sign` (the stand-in's form): fewer steps per term, but a second statement of the pair rule beside the mechanism's; the composition with `pair_terms` is what decision 0220 mapped and costs little at the sizes the tests use.
- For item 7, a shorter message that fits a line: the text is decision 0220's and is pinned by part B's test; changing it would be a change of the interface.
- For item 6, no self-checks: cheaper by one matrix product per tag and one pass per layout, but V4 asks that cheap properties of generated data be checked when generated.
