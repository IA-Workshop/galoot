## Context

Plan 0040 gathered five small changes before the M7 gate: forced inlining of the kernel chain with a structural test, the deletion of `detail::inverse_change`, a deleted `==` on `basis_change` that names `same_rows`, the two `TODO`s of `mv.hpp`, and the compiler-version limit catalogues moved out of the header comments. The plan fixed what each change does; this record states how each was done where the plan left a choice, where the code made the plan's reading wrong, and what the evidence found that the plan did not expect.

## Decision

### The forced inlining (part A)

1. **One macro, on the thirteen functions of the chain and on the thirteen public functions a structure test showed called, and no others.** `GALOOT_ALWAYS_INLINE` in `assert.hpp` is `[[gnu::always_inline]]` under `__GNUC__` or `__clang__` and empty otherwise, with a `///` comment that says what it does, why, what is checked and what is not (below), that it is not part of the interface, and what a caller compiled for fewer instruction-set features than its unit gets (a compile error; the blind review's m1). With the thirteen of the chain alone, the first probe showed no call. The blind review (its M1) then showed that GCC kept the public products out of line where it had inlined them before, and Clang its call at the public function, on motors, flectors and full multivectors, which the first probe did not reach; the delta review (its D1) then showed the same for `project` and `antiproject` once `wedge` and `antiwedge` were forced. Applying plan section 1 item 3's rule to a probe that reaches them, the attribute also goes on `geometric_product`, `wedge`, `geometric_antiproduct`, `antiwedge` and their operators `*`, `^`, `%`, `&`, and on `project` and `antiproject` (called by both compilers), and on `antidot`, `weight` and `left_weight_dual` (called by GCC from a unit of one function, the latter two only once the products were forced). Each of the thirteen is needed on at least one compiler: removing it alone fails a structure test (`records/mutations/0040-pre-release-core-pass/`). The evidence that no other public function needs it is bounded: the probe's 52 functions, each also compiled alone, and two surveys of operations each compiled alone (`records/performance/pre-release-core-pass/survey/`), of which the second, 320 cases on rga3, conformal(3) and R(3,0,0) at `-O2`, `-Os` and `-O3`, finds no rga3 or R(3,0,0) operation called at `-O2` on either compiler. It does find conformal(3) operations called at `-O2` (GCC 12, Clang 14), three of them on GCC new against the branch point (`dot` of even and of full multivectors, `left_bulk_dual` of an even multivector). By the coordinator's ruling (the delta review's option (a), not (b)) the chase stops here: those are not forced, and not claimed. So the unary operations, `+` and `-`, the scalar products, `bulk`, the other duals, `dot`, `change_basis`, `change_basis_back`, the conversion constructor, `truncate` and `part` have no attribute (C12). No `NOLINT` is needed on the macro: clang-tidy's macro check does not flag an attribute.
   - Weighed: bare `[[gnu::always_inline]]` on each function, which repeats a vendor spelling and has no fallback; the attribute on every public operation that calls an attributed function (the delta review's option (b)), which would end the level-by-level chase and cover conformal(3)'s calls, at more `-O0` build cost and with the target-mismatch error reachable from every operation; `[[gnu::flatten]]` on the public operations, which the advisory report found unreliable on Clang.
2. **The structure test reads the compiler's assembly, not the object, in one unit and one function at a time.** `rga3.kernels_inline` runs `test/tools/kernels_inline.py` on `<compiler> ... -S -o - kernels_inline.cpp`; `rga3.kernels_inline_alone` runs it with `--alone`, which compiles each probe function in a unit of its own (the probe's other paragraphs and that function's), because a compiler weighs a unit's growth against its size and GCC kept `antidot` out of line only when alone. The script fails on a call, a jump or a conditional jump whose operand names a symbol of namespace `galoot` anywhere in it, so that `@PLT` and `*sym@GOTPCREL(%rip)` (a `-fno-plt` build) are caught as well as a direct call; on a call through a register an earlier `lea` or `mov` in the function loaded with such a symbol; on the `H` qualifier of an explicit-object member; and on a probe function that the output does not define. It reads x86-64 assembly only. A per-output refusal of assembly that named no x86-64 register (the blind review's m2) misfired twice, on a function that is a bare return (the delta review's D3) and on one built with `-fcf-protection` (the second delta review's E2), so it is dropped: the two structure tests are registered only where `CMAKE_SYSTEM_PROCESSOR` is x86-64, which is the guard and lets another host skip them (the delta review's D5), and the docstring says that output of another target would pass unread. A fixture built as `-fcf-protection` writes it (`endbr64`, and the numeric labels of `.note.gnu.property`) must pass. The address may be loaded with `lea` or as an immediate, into the register called or a narrower part of it, and the branch may carry a `notrack` or `bnd` prefix (the delta review's D4); a register copied or computed is not read, and the docstring says so. The flags are fixed (checking off, `-O2`, `-ffp-contract=off`, the S4 warnings); the include directories and the language standard are read as `galoot_compile_command` reads them, the standard as the last element of its output, which that function documents as last. The probe is also an OBJECT library with each tree's own flags, so that the lint preset sees it; its functions are wrapped in `NOLINTBEGIN/END(misc-use-internal-linkage)`, since they need external linkage to be emitted.
   - Weighed: `nm` on the object, which cannot tell a tail jump from a reference; a fixed list of symbols to forbid, which a new chain function would escape; reading the tree's own flags, which would make the answer depend on the preset (checking on adds calls by design).
3. **The probe: E1's seven functions verbatim and 45 more** (K6, widened after M1 and D1): `sum_lines`, `reverse_line`, `dot_points`, `change_point`, which reach `detail::sum` and `run_sum`, the unary `apply` and `run_map`, `run_product` from `dot`, and `run_change`; and 37 that call each public operation by name and by operator on rga3 motors and flectors and on two multivectors of all sixteen blades, with the unary, sum, dual, change-of-basis and conversion wrappers on the latter; and, after D1, `project` and `antiproject` of full multivectors and `project` of motors and of a motor and a flector. The tag of the changes of basis is a change of rigid(3) to itself that doubles e4, defined in the probe, its value `[[maybe_unused]]` for the units that hold one function alone. Nineteen fixture tests, against the plan's six, give the local-entity form, the conditional jump, the register call, the immediate and narrower-register forms, `notrack`, the overwritten register, the explicit-object form, the bare return with and without `-fcf-protection`, and the four behaviors of `--alone` a test each.
4. **Five attributes the plan held to be guards are needed now.** Removing one attribute at a time, the plan expected `compose`, `transform_middle`, and on GCC `transform` and `run_product` to survive the structure test. They do not: without its attribute, `compose` is called from `compose_motors` on both compilers, `transform` from all three transform probes on GCC, `transform_middle` from `transform_line` on Clang, and, once the public products are forced, `run_product` on GCC too. Forcing a function inline moves its callers' inlining budget, as the advisory report found for `apply`, and the M1 fix is that effect one level further up. Six attributes stay guards (`run_map`, the unary `apply` with a target, `run_sum`, `sum`, `run_change`, `transform_outer`), with `transform_middle` on GCC; the two `apply` overloads with no target layout are reached by no library caller (equivalent). The mutant specs keep the plan's expected verdicts, so `tools/mutate` exits 1 on them, and their README records the observed ones: the record shows the prediction beside the result.
5. **The formatter reads the macro as an attribute** (`c6efd1f`, the merge runner). Without `AttributeMacros: [GALOOT_ALWAYS_INLINE]` in `.clang-format`, clang-format took the macro for the start of a type and indented three of the attributed declarations as continuations. The line only tells the formatter what the token is; no other file's layout changes. It was made outside plan 0040's file table, in its own commit, so that the review can read it alone.
   - Weighed: leaving the formatter's output (three oddly indented declarations); the macro after `[[nodiscard]]`, which formats worse.

### `basis_change` (part B)

6. **The identities test reads the checked inverse**: `core::detail::check_change(change, source, target)`, `REQUIRE`s that its fault is `ChangeFault::none`, and reads `.inverse`. Its references are unchanged (K8).
7. **The deleted `==`**: `bool operator==(const basis_change&) const = delete; // use same_rows(a, b)`, with a `///` comment that says why and what to call. The literal fits the 80 columns with the declaration, so the formatter keeps it on the line that both compilers echo. The compile-fail rows require each compiler's own error text for a deleted function (GCC "use of deleted function", Clang "overload resolution selected deleted operator '=='"), chosen by `CMAKE_CXX_COMPILER_ID`, and the literal `// use same_rows(a, b)`. This supersedes the last sentence of decision 0235 item 1, which declared no `==` because `= delete("…")` needs C++26: a deleted declaration with a comment needs no message argument (decision 0236 item 3).
8. **P8 for part B was the whole suite**, not only the plan's `^core\.basis_change` selection, because the rga3 preset includes `core.hpp`, which includes `basis_change.hpp`.

### The build cost (W10) and O2

13. **The campaign uses the measure trees for "release"** (plan section 1 item 6, K7), and its seven units and the knob criterion are those plan 0040 section 9 fixed before any run. It ran three times: at `7a27f76` (`records/performance/pre-release-core-pass/cost/`), after the M1 fix at `a6f3196` (`cost-public-ops/`), and after the D1 fix at `d2568e3` (`cost-d1/`, on a loaded machine, so its wall times are not comparable and its instruction counts are). **The criterion is met every time.** At `a6f3196`, and within 0.3 points at `d2568e3`, six debug units grow by more than twice the larger range of their repeats and by more than 5%: on GCC `product_test.cpp` +80.5%, `transform_test.cpp` +35.8%, `chains_test.cpp` +17.4% and E1's probe +8.4%; on Clang `product_test.cpp` +17.5% and `chains_test.cpp` +5.0%. GCC's debug compile of `product_test.cpp` takes 4.32 s against 2.18 s and 830 MiB against 418 MiB. As the plan says, no knob is added: the numbers go to the owner as O2, whose recorded default, (a) no knob, stands until the owner rules.

### Lint of a user's code

14. **Two parameter packs renamed for a user's lint** (at the coordinator's request). clang-tidy's `misc-confusable-identifiers`, which the lint preset runs as an error, reported `run_sum`'s pack `I` as confusable with a user's namespace-scope variable `l`, which the documentation lane's examples hit. It was first renamed `Is` (`d2568e3`), which the second delta review (its E1) showed is confusable with a user's `ls`: the check reduces names to a skeleton in which `I` and `l` are one letter. The packs are now `Xs` and `Ys` (the partner `J` renamed too, so that the pair reads as the terms of x and of y), names with none of `I`, `l`, `1`, `O`, `0`, `rn` or `m`. Token comparisons show only the pack names and one source line number changed: six `I` to `Is`, then six `Is` to `Xs` and six `J` to `Ys`; and, since the second fold of `run_sum` was rewrapped onto two lines, the `__LINE__` that one later assertion of `linear.hpp` passes moved from 415 to 416 (the third delta review's m1), which changes no behavior. `core_linear_user_names`, an OBJECT library compiled in every build, names lines `l` and `ls` at namespace scope, so the lint preset fails if either clash comes back; it does not name `I` or `rn`, see below. A scan of 114 short user names (the second delta review's list, with `Xs`, `Ys`, `xs`, `ys`, `Xx`, `Yy` added) finds two clashes left, both with header variables, not packs, which are reported and not renamed: `l` in `basis_change.hpp` against a user's `I`, and `m` in `bits.hpp` against a user's `rn`.

### After gate 2: the opt-out of the forced inlining

16. **`GALOOT_FORCE_INLINE`, a public macro that turns the attribute off, set in four presets** (plan 0040 section 18; the owner's ruling on the gate 2 result, decision 0245 item 1 reopened, recommendation (a)). Gate 2 ran the P9 matrix at `a310a2f`. With the twenty-six attributes of item 1, the identity test units cost far more in the sanitizer and the optimized Clang builds than at `ed9288f`: gcc-asan took 5,367.6 s against 248.4 s, with `identities_rounding_test.cpp` at 5,302.8 s and `identities_four_test.cpp` at 22.0 GB peak; clang-asan was stopped at 216 of 237 build steps with 8 to 16 GB per compile and 8 GiB of the machine left; clang-release took 665.1 s against 118.5 s (`identities_rounding_test.cpp` 600.0 s) and clang-measure 666.0 s against 116.1 s. gcc-debug, gcc-release, clang-debug, lint and gcc-measure stayed within about 1.6 times their `ed9288f` times.
   - The form follows `GALOOT_CHECKING`'s: a value of 0 or 1, 1 by default, and any other value an `#error` with one literal message. With 0, `GALOOT_ALWAYS_INLINE` expands to nothing. `GALOOT_ALWAYS_INLINE` itself stays outside the interface; `GALOOT_FORCE_INLINE` is public and documented in the README and the core guide (its own section and a reference entry).
   - The build carries it as the cache option `GALOOT_FORCE_INLINE` (default ON). OFF passes `GALOOT_FORCE_INLINE=0` through `galoot::core`'s build interface, as the checking mode is passed; ON passes nothing, so that a test can fix the mode 0 on its own target in any preset (an identical second definition is no error on either compiler, a different one is under `-Werror`). Unlike `GALOOT_CHECKING`, it has a default, because no test checks a value that depends on it. gcc-asan, clang-asan, clang-release and clang-measure set it OFF, and their display names say so. gcc-measure is left on, since the gate showed no outlier there; so are the two `-O3` measure presets, which the gate does not run.
   - The structure tests `rga3.kernels_inline` and `rga3.kernels_inline_alone` build with fixed flags and only the core's include directories, so they check the attribute in every preset, the four included.
   - Tests: `core.force_inline_off`, `_default` and `_build` (what the attribute macro expands to with the mode 0, with no mode, and with the build's mode, the last told its expected mode apart from the mode itself); `core.force_inline_value` (the value 2 is one error with the literal); `rga3.reference_kernels_unforced` (the reference-kernel test built with the mode 0, so that the operations give the reference values, and for a transform the same bits, without the attribute).
   - Weighed: no knob (decision 0245 item 1 as first written), which leaves the gate's sanitizer builds at hours and tens of gigabytes; `-DGALOOT_FORCE_INLINE=0` written into each of the four presets' flag strings, which needs no CMake code but reaches the targets that fix their own mode, so a test of the default would have to undo it; the opt-out on the identity test targets alone, in every preset, which keeps the benchmarks of clang-measure forced but hides a per-target exception in the build and was not the ruling; a name in the negative (`GALOOT_NO_FORCE_INLINE`, defined or not), which reads as on when given the value 0.
   - Cost with the opt-out (`records/performance/pre-release-core-pass/opt-out/`, at `2d292ef`): the six units of the gate's evidence compile under gcc-asan, clang-release and clang-asan with the instruction counts and peak memory of `ed9288f`, to within 1.3%. The largest compile is `identities_four_test.cpp` under gcc-asan, 1,777 MiB against 21,519 MiB at the gate; `identities_rounding_test.cpp` under gcc-asan retires 375 × 10⁹ instructions against 47,076 × 10⁹, and under clang-release 120 × 10⁹ against 4,275 × 10⁹.

## Consequences

- **What is claimed.** Each of the 52 functions of `test/bench/kernels_inline.cpp` compiles to the arithmetic of its terms at its call site, with no call or jump into the library, on GCC 16.2.1 and Clang 23.1.4 at `-O2` with the structure test's flags (checking off, `-ffp-contract=off`, x86-64), both inside the probe's one unit and alone in a unit of its own; `rga3.kernels_inline` and `rga3.kernels_inline_alone` fail on the compiler that makes such a call. The 52 are E1's seven operations, which are DoD-24's; a sum of lines, the reverse of a line, the dot of two points and a change of basis of a point; the geometric product of two motors by name and by `*`, their antiproduct by name, the antiproduct of a motor and a flector by name and by `%`, and all four products of two rga3 multivectors of all sixteen blades by name and by operator; and each unary operation, sum, difference, dual, dot, antidot, projection, change of basis and conversion of the latter, with the projections also on a point and a plane, on motors, and on a motor and a flector; all over `double`. The second survey adds, as evidence and not as a test, that none of its 320 cases on rga3 and R(3,0,0) calls at `-O2` on either compiler at `390b55e`.
- **What is not claimed.** Other layouts and other algebras: on conformal(3) multivectors of all or of even blades, `bulk`, the duals that reach it, `dot`, `project` and `antiproject` (GCC) and the unary operations, `bulk`, three duals, `+`, `-` and `dot` of `full<5>` (Clang) still compile to a call at `-O2`, three of them on GCC new against the branch point. Other optimization levels: at `-Os` both compilers call rga3 operations, among them probe functions on Clang; at `-O3` no rga3 case calls in the survey, but that level is not tested. Checking builds, where `transform` calls its checks out of line by design. Other scalar types and other compilers or versions.
- A caller compiled for fewer instruction-set features than its unit cannot call a forced operation (m1); the macro's comment says so and how to avoid it. Forcing the public products, `project` and `antiproject` makes this reachable from more call sites: such a caller of `project` compiled at `414879e` and does not compile now.
- At `-O0` each call of a forced function carries its body: E1's probe's text grows 12.0% on GCC and 1.8% on Clang, against the branch point; at `-O2` it shrinks (GCC -0.4%, Clang -8.8%).
- `a == b` and `a != b` on two `basis_change` values fail to compile with one error whose note names `same_rows`.
- In the presets gcc-asan, clang-asan, clang-release and clang-measure the operations are not forced inline (item 16). The suites in those presets check values with the compiler's own inlining, and what clang-measure's benchmarks and compiled code show is the code of a build with `GALOOT_FORCE_INLINE` 0, not the default a user gets; a measurement of the forced code reads gcc-measure, or configures clang-measure with `-DGALOOT_FORCE_INLINE=ON`.
- The header comments no longer state compiler-version sizes; until the documentation lane's `docs/guide/limits.md` is on `develop`, their pointers name a heading of a page not yet there (plan 0040 risk 6, K10).
